# Why the study examines these 61 checks

This supplement explains the scope and purpose of the retained checklist. It is a retrospective interpretation of the recorded study, not a claim that a systematic search proved these to be the best 61 tests. Reference numbers refer to the paper's bibliography. The exact requests and pass rules are in Appendix B.

## Documented origin and scope

The development record describes bringing an AI-standards checklist and a general website checklist together. Source revisions dated 28 August 2026 contain 32, then 52, then 61 distinct check identifiers. The final expansion explicitly names AI search, agents, SEO, and citations as its scope [34]. The 1 September revision used to document the study retains the same 61 identifiers.

`data/checklist_history.json` records the revisions, messages, dates, source hashes, check identifiers, and category counts. `verify_checklist_history.cjs` can retrieve the immutable revisions and recount the identifiers without executing their code. The change from 52 to 61 adds 13 identifiers and removes four. An identifier change alone does not show that a wholly new capability was added; it can also reflect renaming or regrouping.

The practical scope is public files, response text, headers, and a few input-derived values that can be checked without accounts, browser rendering, form submission, or payment execution. The six categories cover finding resources, obtaining content, locating service interfaces, general security context, descriptive metadata, and payment-related signals. The original scope therefore extends beyond the requirements of an autonomous agent that performs actions.

The number also depends on how observations are grouped. Basic MCP-response detection and the capabilities-field test are separate checks, while the GPTBot- and PerplexityBot-labelled requests produce one combined check. The 61 items are neither independent requests nor 61 necessary conditions for every agent task. Other checks, including some that could also be performed on public responses, remain possible.

## Check-by-check purpose and limits

“Format-based” means that a published format or protocol gives the observation a recognizable purpose. It does not mean this implementation validates that format. “Context” means a general website property whose importance depends on the task. “Exploratory” or “heuristic” means that the particular filename, pattern, or cutoff is an engineering choice rather than an established readiness requirement. None of these labels establishes predictive accuracy for agent-task success.

## What this justifies

The mapping gives a concrete reason to inspect each observation within the original broad checklist. It also shows why their relevance differs. Public access and interface-format checks address possible routes to information or services. General website metadata and security headers provide context. Payment and integration-specific checks apply only to some uses. Exploratory paths and loose text rules remain tentative observations.

This explains the inventory's engineering scope and the choice to report it in full. It is not evidence that the selection is exhaustive, that the thresholds are optimal, or that the total score predicts successful agent behavior. Those stronger claims need separate validation.
