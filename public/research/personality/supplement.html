Formal sensitivity methods

Numeric agreement averages the fraction of equal unordered answer pairs within each repeatedly numeric item. An item with n numeric answers has n(n-1)/2 such pairs. Applicability agreement applies the same pair calculation to numeric-versus-null flags over all fifty items and requires at least two usable maps. For the category-spacing check, F_T(k) is the observed fraction of keyed ratings in trait T at or below category k, using joint-complete responses. First-order dominance over Extraversion requires F_T(k) <= F_E(k) for k=1,2,3,4, with at least one strict inequality. For any strictly increasing recoding g, mean(g(T))-mean(g(E)) = sum over k=1..4 of [g(k+1)-g(k)]*[F_E(k)-F_T(k)]. Positive category increments and nonnegative cumulative margins therefore preserve the larger observed mean. The recoding applies to keyed categories after reverse scoring; it does not calibrate latent psychological traits.

Calculation of missing-answer limits

For known keyed sum S, u unknown positions, and R planned attempts, the lower and upper possible domain averages are (S+u)/(10R) and (S+5u)/(10R). Unknown positions include explicit nulls and all positions in unusable requests. These limits target a hypothetical average over all planned positions, distinct from the reported mean over complete-domain occasions. They do not assume a non-applicable answer conceals a true numeric rating. A direction is guaranteed under this coding only when one domain's lower limit exceeds the other's upper limit. This question differs from recoding observed categories; passing the two checks separately does not imply simultaneous invariance to both changes.
