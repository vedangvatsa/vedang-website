<SectionLabel label="Deploy Day" />

Your app is polished. It's secure. It does one thing well. Now it needs to live on the internet where real people can use it.

You're going to get a live URL in the next 90 minutes.

### The Stack

Here's what you need and where to get it:

**Vercel for your frontend.** Connect your GitHub repo. Push your code. Vercel deploys it automatically. Every time you push a change, it redeploys. Free tier is generous enough for your first 10,000 users. If you built with Lovable or Replit, they have their own deploy buttons - use those.

**Supabase for auth and database.** Email magic links for login (no passwords to manage). PostgreSQL database. Real-time subscriptions if you need live updates. Row Level Security so users can only see their own data. The free tier handles 50,000 monthly active users.

**Stripe for payments.** Don't set this up until you have users who want to pay. When you're ready: start in test mode, build your checkout flow, verify it works, then flip to live mode. Stripe handles taxes, invoices, and subscriptions so you don't have to.

**Custom domain.** Buy one on Namecheap or Cloudflare (~$10/year for a .com). Point it at Vercel by updating your DNS records. This takes 10 minutes and makes your app look legitimate instead of living at some-random-hash.vercel.app.

**Analytics.** Install Plausible or PostHog. Both are privacy-friendly (no cookie banners needed). They tell you what people actually do in your app - which pages they visit, where they drop off, which features they use. Don't guess. Measure.

**Error tracking.** Install Sentry. When your app crashes for a user in Tokyo at 3am, Sentry captures the error, the stack trace, and the user's browser info. You'll know about problems before your users email you about them.

<Callout type="info" title="The Deploy Checklist">
1. Push code to GitHub (or use your tool's built-in deploy)
2. Connect to Vercel (or Replit/Lovable deploy)
3. Set environment variables in the hosting dashboard
4. Add your custom domain
5. Install analytics (Plausible or PostHog)
6. Install error tracking (Sentry)
7. Test the live URL on your phone
8. Send the link to one friend

If any step takes more than 15 minutes, you're overcomplicating it. Ask Claude or your AI tool for help.
</Callout>

<SectionLabel label="User Testing Without Mercy" />

Your app is live. Now comes the hard part: watching real people use it.

This will be uncomfortable. That's how you know it's working.

### Step 1 - Recruit 10 Testers

Friends, coworkers, people on Reddit, people on X/Twitter. DM them directly. Don't post a generic "check out my app!" and hope for the best. Message specific people who fit your target user profile.

Offer nothing in return. People who want free stuff or Amazon gift cards aren't your target users. You want people who have the actual problem your app solves. They'll test it because they're curious whether it works, not because you bribed them.

### Step 2 - Give Them ONE Task

Don't send a 5-page instruction manual. Send one sentence:

> "Sign up and [complete core action]."

That's it. "Sign up and generate your first proposal." "Sign up and upload a photo." "Sign up and create your first project." One task. One sentence.

Don't explain how to do it. Don't show them around. Don't say "the button is in the top right corner." If they can't figure it out, that's not their failure. That's your design failure. And you need to know about it.

### Step 3 - Watch Them

The best option: sit next to them and watch over their shoulder. Don't help. Don't explain. Just watch and take notes.

The second best option: ask them to record their screen with Loom while they use it. Watch the recording later.

Note every moment of confusion. Every time they hesitate. Every time they click the wrong thing. Every time they say "wait, what does this do?" Write it all down.

### Step 4 - Analyze the Patterns

After 5 sessions, you'll start seeing the same problems over and over. Three people couldn't find the submit button. Four people didn't understand what the app does from the landing page. Two people got an error on the results screen.

These patterns are gold. They tell you exactly what to fix next.

<Callout type="insight" title="The Steve Krug Rule">
Don't ask users what they want. Watch what they do. The gap between what people say and what people do is where all the insights live. Someone will say "the app is great" and then struggle for 45 seconds to find the main button. Believe their actions, not their words.
</Callout>

<SectionLabel label="Build in Public" />

In 2026, distribution is the hard part. Not building. Building is the easy part now - AI handles that. Getting people to know your app exists is the real challenge.

Your content IS your marketing. Your build log IS your distribution. Every day you build, you should be posting about it.

### Why This Matters

The indie builders making real money right now are the ones who share their journey. Pieter Levels built 70+ startups, generates $3M+ ARR, and tweets everything - his revenue, his failures, his tech stack decisions. Marc Lou shipped 20+ products in public and built a massive following in the process.

They're not famous because they built great products. They built great audiences by sharing the building process, and then the audience made their products successful.

### The Daily Post Formula

Every day you work on your app, post this:

1. **What I shipped today** - one specific thing. "Added dark mode" or "Fixed the mobile layout" or "Launched the pricing page."
2. **Screenshot or video** - people scroll past text. They stop for visuals. Screen recordings of your app in action are the best performing content.
3. **One thing I learned** - a lesson, a mistake, a surprising insight. "Learned that 80% of my users are on mobile. Rewrote the whole layout."
4. **What's next** - creates anticipation. Brings people back tomorrow.

That's it. Four elements. Takes 10 minutes to write.

### Platform Strategy

- **X/Twitter** - Best for developer and startup audiences. Short, punchy posts. Screenshots do well. Revenue numbers do very well.
- **LinkedIn** - Best for B2B buyers and professional audiences. Longer storytelling format. "Here's what I learned building X" performs better than product announcements.
- **TikTok** - Best for consumer apps. Show the app in action. 15-second demo videos. Conversational tone. Don't be corporate.

You don't need to be on all three. Pick the one where your target users spend time. If you're building for developers, that's X. If you're building for business professionals, that's LinkedIn. If you're building for consumers, that's TikTok.

### AI-Powered Content

You're building with AI. Use AI for your marketing too.

- Use Claude to turn your daily dev log into 3 platform-specific posts
- Use Opus Clip to chop demo videos into short-form clips
- Use your app's screenshots as content - people love seeing products get built in real time

<SectionLabel label="The Growth Sprint" />

Your goal this week: **25 users.** Not 25,000. Twenty-five real humans who signed up and used your app.

Here's how:

**Post in relevant subreddits.** Don't spam. Find the subreddit where your target users hang out. Add value first - answer questions, help people, be useful. Then share your app as something you built to solve the problem people keep asking about.

**DM 10 people who fit your target user profile.** Go on X or LinkedIn. Find 10 people who publicly talk about the problem your app solves. Send them a short message. No pitch deck. No long explanation. Just: "Hey, I built something that might help with [their specific problem]. Want to try it?"

**Launch on Product Hunt.** Even a soft launch gets eyeballs. You don't need to be #1. You just need exposure. The Product Hunt community is full of early adopters who love trying new tools.

**Ask every tester to invite one friend.** After someone uses your app and says they like it, ask: "Do you know one other person who has this same problem?" One warm introduction is worth more than 100 cold signups.

You are a social media strategist for indie builders. I just [WHAT YOU DID TODAY] on my app [APP NAME] which [ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION OF WHAT IT DOES].

Write 3 posts:

1. Twitter/X post (280 chars max, punchy, include a hook in the first line)
2. LinkedIn post (3 short paragraphs: the story of what I built today, the lesson I learned, what's next. Professional but human tone.)
3. TikTok script (15 seconds, conversational, starts with a hook like "I just built something wild" or "Day [X] of building my app")

Include relevant hashtags for each platform. Don't use buzzwords. Sound like a real person, not a marketing department.
</PromptTemplate>

<PromptTemplate title="UX Researcher" description="Analyze your user testing sessions and find the patterns.">
You are a UX researcher. Here are my notes from [NUMBER] user testing sessions for my app [APP NAME].

[PASTE YOUR SESSION NOTES HERE]

Analyze these sessions and give me:

1. Top 3 usability issues ranked by frequency (how many users hit each one)
2. The single biggest blocker to task completion
3. Any contradictions between what users said and what they actually did
4. Three specific fixes I should ship this week, ordered by impact
5. One thing that's working well that I should NOT change

Be direct. Don't sugarcoat the problems. I need to know what's broken so I can fix it.
</PromptTemplate>

By the end of today, real people should be using your app on the real internet. That's not a stretch goal. That's the minimum.
