In 2016, the security firm Imperva published a finding that has since become the most cited statistic in the bot traffic literature. More than half of all web traffic was generated by bots, not humans. The finding was based on Imperva's own network data, and it held roughly steady for years.

The 2024 numbers published by Cloudflare suggest the balance has shifted further. Cloudflare's network processes an average of 57 million HTTP requests per second across more than 330 cities. From that vantage, the company published a detailed breakdown of AI crawler activity in July 2024, updated with a new bot taxonomy in July 2026. This piece compiles those primary sources with additional data from Cloudflare Radar, Wikipedia, and public crawler documentation.

## The top AI crawlers, by request volume

In July 2024, [Cloudflare published](https://blog.cloudflare.com/declaring-your-aindependence-block-ai-bots-scrapers-and-crawlers-with-a-single-click/) a network-wide survey of AI crawler traffic. The top four crawlers by request volume were Bytespider, operated by ByteDance, the Chinese owner of TikTok. Amazonbot, reportedly used to index content for Alexa. ClaudeBot, Anthropic's crawler used to train the Claude models. GPTBot, OpenAI's crawler used to train its GPT models.

Bytespider accessed 40.40 percent of websites protected by Cloudflare. GPTBot accessed 35.46 percent. ClaudeBot accessed 11.17 percent. ImagesiftBot, a lesser-known crawler associated with image-search training, accessed 8.75 percent. CCBot, the Common Crawl bot, accessed 2.14 percent.

The same Cloudflare analysis found that among the top one million internet properties using its network, 38.73 percent were accessed by AI bots. Only 2.98 percent of those properties took any action to block or challenge those requests. The higher-ranked an internet property was, the more likely it was to be targeted and the more likely to block. Among the top ten sites by traffic, 80 percent were accessed by AI bots and 40 percent blocked them. Among the top 100, 63 percent accessed, 16 percent blocked. The slope is clear: the largest sites have both the most to lose and the resources to act.

## Bytespider, the largest crawler, has been declining

[Cloudflare's 2024 Year in Review](https://blog.cloudflare.com/radar-2024-year-in-review/), published in December 2024, tracked the trajectory of the top AI crawlers across the full year. Bytespider's crawling activity trended downward. By the end of November 2024, its activity was approximately 80 to 85 percent lower than at the start of the year. ClaudeBot, in contrast, showed almost no activity through mid-April 2024, then grew consistently starting in late April, with an early peak in May and June before trailing off through the remainder of the year.

The downward arc for Bytespider does not necessarily mean ByteDance stopped crawling. Cloudflare noted that its bot detection classifies traffic based on observed patterns, and bot operators frequently change user agents and request profiles to avoid detection. A decline in identified Bytespider traffic could mean less crawling, or it could mean better evasion.

## AI crawlers spoof browsers

The same Cloudflare analysis found that bot operators attempt to appear as real web browsers by using spoofed user agents. A specific bot, which the company did not name in the report but which other researchers identified as Perplexity, had been observed to hide its activity behind browser-like user agent strings. Cloudflare's global machine learning model scored this traffic as likely bot, with scores concentrated below 9 on a 1-to-99 scale, regardless of the spoofed user agent. The user agent changes had no effect on the score because Cloudflare treats user-agent switching as the first thing a dishonest bot operator would do.

Any Cloudflare customer with a Web Application Firewall rule set to challenge visitors with a bot score below 30 automatically blocked this traffic. Customers without such rules, which is most customers on the free and lower-tier plans, did not.

## The economic value of crawled content

The economics of web content licensing began shifting in 2024. [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/technology/reddit-ai-content-licensing-deal-with-google-sources-say-2024-02-22/) in February 2024 that Google was paying Reddit approximately $60 million per year to license user-generated content for AI training. Other licensing deals followed. OpenAI signed agreements with publishers including the Financial Times, Axel Springer, and Le Monde. The deals set a market price for content access, but they cover only the largest publishers. The long tail of the web, the millions of small and medium websites that Cloudflare protects, was being crawled without compensation by the same AI companies that were writing cheques to Reddit and the FT.

Cloudflare's one-click "Block AI Scrapers and Crawlers" button, launched in July 2024, was a direct response to this asymmetry. The feature gave every customer, including those on the free tier, the ability to block all known AI crawlers with a single toggle. By that point, Cloudflare said, its customers overwhelmingly chose to block AI crawlers when given the option.

## The July 2026 taxonomy update

In [July 2026](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/concepts/bot/verified-bots/), Cloudflare updated its bot classification taxonomy with a new set of behavior labels. The most significant change was the elimination of the distinction between "AI Search" and traditional search crawlers. Both are now classified under a single Search behavior. Training crawlers, such as GPTBot and ClaudeBot, remain a separate Training classification. A new Agent classification covers user-directed agents that visit a page on behalf of a human.

Cloudflare also introduced a distinction between Direct and Intermediary bots. Direct bots are operated by a single, narrow operator on the operator's own infrastructure. Intermediary bots are agentic services that a wide range of end users can operate. The operator runs the software, but each action is initiated by a different end user. Because the operator and the end user are not the same party, the taxonomy introduces transitive trust: a website owner may trust the intermediary operator but not every end user driving it.

The taxonomy update also documented a new verification method, [Web Bot Auth](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/reference/bot-verification/web-bot-auth/), a cryptographic signature mechanism for bots to prove their identity deterministically. The update marks a shift from user-agent-based trust, which is trivially spoofed, to cryptographic attestation that a request genuinely originates from the claimed bot.

## What is current and what is not

Two primary sources date the data in this piece. The [Cloudflare 2025 Year in Review](https://blog.cloudflare.com/radar-2025-year-in-review/) covers 1 January through 2 December 2025. The [Cloudflare Verified Bots documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/concepts/bot/verified-bots/) was last updated 1 July 2026 and reflects the current classification taxonomy. Data points that are eight months old as of publication are labelled as 2025 observations. Data points that reflect the current taxonomy or product state as of mid-2026 are labelled accordingly. The piece does not claim real-time precision for the traffic shares, which shift week to week and season to season. The trend directions and the orders of magnitude are the durable findings.

The [2024 Cloudflare Year in Review](https://blog.cloudflare.com/radar-2024-year-in-review/) also reported that the United States was responsible for over a third of global bot traffic. Amazon Web Services was the single largest source, accounting for 12.7 percent of global bot traffic. Google accounted for 7.8 percent. These figures include all bot traffic, not only AI crawlers. The concentration shows that bot traffic does not diffuse evenly across the internet. It clusters on the infrastructure that can afford the compute to run it and the bandwidth to sustain it.

A separate finding from the same report showed that Go surpassed Node.js as the most popular language for making automated API requests in 2024. Go's share increased by approximately 40 percent, while Node.js fell by just over 30 percent. The language shift matters because Go is heavily used in cloud infrastructure and microservices, the environments where agents and crawlers run. The API traffic that Go clients generate is increasingly agent-driven, not browser-driven.

The Year in Review also found that 20.5 percent of web requests used HTTP/3 in 2024, and that 6.5 percent of global traffic was mitigated by Cloudflare as potentially malicious or for customer-defined reasons. The mitigated share varies by country. In the United States it was 5.1 percent. In 44 countries, it exceeded 10 percent. The gap reflects both different threat profiles and different levels of customer adoption of Cloudflare's security products.

## The broader historical context

The Wikipedia entry for [Internet bot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_bot) cites Imperva's 2016 finding that more than half of all web traffic is generated by bots, and notes that 94.2 percent of websites have experienced a bot attack. The entry was last updated in August 2026 and reflects the classification systems that were in place before Cloudflare's taxonomy change.

The long arc of bot traffic on the web runs from search engine crawlers in the 1990s, through spam and DDoS bots in the 2000s, to the current wave of AI training crawlers and agent-driven traffic. Each wave prompted a defensive response. The robots.txt standard dates to 1994. CAPTCHAs entered widespread use in the early 2000s. Cloudflare's one-click AI crawler block and Web Bot Auth represent the 2024-2026 generation of defences. Each generation of defence is specific to the generation of bots that provoked it.

## What is not yet measured

The Cloudflare data covers the portion of the internet behind its network, which is large but not universal. The data does not capture traffic that never reaches Cloudflare, traffic inside private networks, or traffic between agents that communicate through APIs without touching a web server.

Three things would improve the public understanding. A regular, independent census of AI crawler traffic across multiple CDN and hosting networks, not only Cloudflare. Published breakdowns of API traffic by request source, distinguishing agent-driven calls from browser-driven calls and mobile app calls. A longitudinal study of the commercial terms under which content is licensed versus crawled, and whether the Reddit-Google model of paid licences expands or contracts over time.

The Cloudflare data shows that the agentic internet is already here, and that the human internet is a minority use of web infrastructure. What is not clear is at what scale the agentic internet is welcome, and at what scale it is an extraction.
