The largest AI compute projects of the past two years are financed with sovereign money, convertible instruments, and bank debt. The public discussion treats the announcements as investment. Three separate numbers tell different stories: announced, funded, and spent. They rarely match. The instrument in each case shows who holds the risk.

## Stargate: $500 billion announced, $38 billion funded, not started

Stargate was [announced at the White House on 21 January 2025](https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/openai-softbank-oracle-invest-500-bln-ai-trump-says-2025-01-21/) as a joint venture of OpenAI, SoftBank, Oracle, and MGX, with plans to spend up to $500 billion on US AI infrastructure by 2029. The funding structure was [reported by The Information on 23 January 2025](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/openai-softbank-each-commit-19-billion-to-stargate-data-center-venture).

The debt structure matters more than the headline. [The Wall Street Journal reported in April 2025](https://www.wsj.com/business/deals/openai-softbank-investment-debt-51b4a130) that SoftBank, the main financial backer, was expected to supply only about 10% equity. The first $10 billion of its commitment was expected to come from Mizuho and other Japanese lenders. In May 2025, [JPMorgan agreed to lend $2.3 billion](https://commercialobserver.com/2025/01/j-p-morgan-chase-loan-texas-data-center-oracle/) for the Abilene, Texas data centre project, which is leased to Oracle.

On 7 August 2025, [Bloomberg reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-07/softbank-concedes-stargate-project-with-openai-needs-more-time) that the project had not started and no funds had been raised to meet the $500 billion budget. SoftBank conceded it needed more time.

Then the announcements resumed. [OpenAI said on 23 September 2025](https://openai.com/index/five-new-stargate-sites/) that Stargate would add five new data centre sites, taking planned capacity to nearly 7 gigawatts and claiming over $400 billion in investment over three years. In October 2025, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/openai-sur-energy-weigh-25-billion-argentina-data-center-project-2025-10-10/) a $25 billion, 500-megawatt project for Patagonia, Argentina, with Sur Energy.

The September announcement was about an infrastructure plan. The August Bloomberg report was about money raised. The instruments between them, a 10% equity base with 90% bank debt, mean the lenders hold the exposure when construction hasn't started.

## MGX: $100 billion target, $40 billion spent, $50 billion raised

MGX is the Abu Dhabi state investment vehicle for AI, launched in March 2024 with Mubadala and G42 and a target of $100 billion in assets under management ([Bloomberg, 11 Mar 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-03-11/abu-dhabi-said-to-target-100-billion-aum-for-ai-investment-firm)). Its chair is Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed, the UAE's national security adviser.

The vehicle's transactions are a ledger. In September 2024, MGX joined BlackRock and Microsoft in the Global AI Infrastructure Investment Partnership for data centres and power infrastructure ([WSJ, 17 Sep 2024](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/blackrock-global-infrastructure-partners-microsoft-mgx-launch-ai-partnership-1d00e09f)). In January 2025 it took a Stargate stake. In October 2025, a consortium of MGX, AIP, and BlackRock's Global Infrastructure Partners agreed to buy Aligned Data Centers for $40 billion, [reported as the largest data centre deal to date](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-15/blackrock-s-gip-buys-aligned-data-centers-in-40-billion-ai-bet). In June 2026, [Bloomberg reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-23/abu-dhabi-s-mgx-raises-about-50-billion-to-accelerate-ai-deals) that MGX had raised close to $50 billion for an AI infrastructure fund.

A $100 billion target in 2024. A $40 billion acquisition in 2025. A $50 billion fund raise in 2026. The acquisition is a spent item. The fund raise is a funded item. What is not public is the portion deployed versus reserved, because MGX does not publish a portfolio ledger.

## G42: licences for compute, with debt-like strings

G42 is the Emirati AI group chaired by Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G42_(company))). Its history shows the exchange at the centre of sovereign AI: capital and compute access against regulatory clearance.

In July 2023, G42 agreed to pay about $100 million for the first of up to nine Cerebras supercomputers ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/cerebras-systems-signs-100-mln-ai-supercomputer-deal-with-uaes-g42-2023-07-20/)). In April 2024, Microsoft [announced a $1.5 billion investment](https://www.ft.com/content/930a1c17-2e84-4ddb-8e72-4e4a1b833c40) in G42, with Microsoft's president joining the board. US lawmakers had asked for an intelligence assessment of G42's China ties before the deal could advance, and the company said it had divested its China holdings.

The licences followed. In November 2025, the US Department of Commerce authorised the export of advanced semiconductors equivalent to 35,000 NVIDIA Blackwell chips to G42. In July 2026, the US reclassified the UAE to Country Group A:5 and designated G42 as an authorised receiving entity, meaning eligible exporters could ship certain advanced AI chips without individual licences. A company under scrutiny for China ties in 2023 became a designated US-licensed recipient by 2026.

The European pledges scale with the licences. In June 2025, G42 launched its Europe unit with pledges of up to $52 billion for French data centres and $40 billion for Italy. In May 2025, it joined the [Stargate UAE project](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/22/stargate-uae-openai-nvidia-oracle.html) with OpenAI, Oracle, NVIDIA, SoftBank, and Cisco. In November 2025, [Microsoft and G42 announced a 200-megawatt data centre expansion](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/microsoft-g42-announce-200-mw-data-centre-capacity-expansion-uae-2025-11-05/) in the UAE. In February 2026, a Vietnamese consortium framework for up to $1 billion, and a national-scale supercomputer for India.

The French and Italian pledges are letters of intent attached to licence authorisations. G42 does not publish whether the licence, the pledge, or the ground breaking came first.

## Saudi Arabia: the fund still being shaped

Saudi Arabia sits behind the other vehicles through its Public Investment Fund. In February 2024, the PIF launched Alat with a reported $100 billion allocation for advanced manufacturing and technology. Throughout 2025, outlets reported that the PIF was planning a dedicated AI fund in the range of $40 billion to $100 billion.

The Saudi numbers are still at the planning stage. MGX and G42 have identifiable transactions: Aligned Data Centers, the Cerebras deal, the Microsoft investment, the Stargate equity. The Saudi numbers do not yet have a transaction ledger. The instrument, a sovereign fund allocating state oil revenue to AI compute, is the largest in the set, and the gap between announced and funded is the widest.

## SoftBank: the debt engine

SoftBank is the financial backer of Stargate and, through its ownership of ARM, the company most exposed to the AI infrastructure build-out. The Stargate financing, [reported by the Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/deals/openai-softbank-investment-debt-51b4a130), said SoftBank would supply about 10% equity, with the rest from bank borrowing. The first $10 billion was expected from Mizuho and other Japanese lenders.

The MGX model and the SoftBank model point in opposite directions. MGX raises sovereign capital and deploys it directly, acquiring assets outright. SoftBank borrows and puts the borrowed money into ventures it chairs. The risk in the MGX model sits with the sovereign fund. The risk in the SoftBank model sits with the banks that lent to SoftBank. Both are governments and government-backed firms financing AI compute with instruments that sit closer to debt than to direct investment.

## Who holds the risk

The instruments allocate the downside. Stargate's structure puts the bulk of the money as debt against a small equity base, with SoftBank's reported 10% equity and Mizuho and JPMorgan providing the loans. If the AI hardware market cools, the lenders hold the exposure. The government of Japan, through its banks, is financing US AI infrastructure. The government of Abu Dhabi, through MGX, is acquiring data centres outright, which means it owns the assets if the leases hold.

The Sarvam pattern from the [IndiaAI piece](/indiaai) is the smaller version. A government supplies subsidised compute and receives convertibles that may become 1-2% of the company. MGX and G42 are the national version, where the state supplies capital and licences and receives either equity or infrastructure ownership. In both cases, the accountability question is the same: what happens to the public interest if the assets underperform.

## What would close the gap

Three documents.

1. **Deployment schedules with dates.** Stargate's five sites have announcement dates. Ground-breaking and power-connection dates would show progress.
2. **Equity-to-debt ratios at close.** The [WSJ report](https://www.wsj.com/business/deals/openai-softbank-investment-debt-51b4a130) on SoftBank's 10% equity stake is the most important single number in this piece. Published capital structures would make every project comparable.
3. **Portfolio holdings with valuations.** MGX's $50 billion fund has a target. A published list of deployed capital at cost would separate raised from spent.

Announcements are free and ground breaking is not. Until the sovereign funds publish what they spent rather than what they pledged, the AI build-out is a credit market with press releases, and the risk sits with whoever financed the last tranche.
