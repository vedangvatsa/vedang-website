Bitget says unauthorized transfers affected approximately **$351.6 million** across parts of its hot and warm wallet infrastructure on September 24, 2026. The exchange has temporarily suspended withdrawals while it reviews security. In a [statement on X](https://x.com/GracyBitget/status/2103235655879074084), CEO Gracy Chen said cold wallets remain secure and the exchange's User Protection Fund, which she put at more than **$464 million**, covers the estimated loss.

<Figure src="/images/essays/bitget-hack.svg" alt="Bitget's September 24 statement distinguishes affected hot and warm wallets, paused withdrawals, and an investigation with no disclosed attack method." caption="Status described by Bitget's CEO in her September 24 security notice. Security and fund-coverage assurances are company statements." />

Chen said Bitget detected the unauthorized transfers at **18:31 UTC** and activated its emergency response. Deposits and trading remain operational, according to the notice. She did not announce a time for withdrawals to resume.

The statement confirms that Bitget regards the transfers as unauthorized, following earlier reports of suspicious movements from exchange-labelled addresses. It does not establish the final loss, explain how the attacker gained access, or provide an independent assessment of the remaining wallets.

*This report covers the September 24 incident and the CEO's notice published at 21:30 UTC. Operational status and estimates may change as Bitget releases further updates.*

Chen described a three-tier wallet architecture and said the breach affected only a portion of its hot and warm wallet layers. Her statement expressly says cold wallets remain secure. The distinction matters because some early public alerts referred to movements from addresses labelled as cold wallets.

Bitget says its team identified, flagged and reported addresses associated with abnormal transfers. It also says law enforcement and blockchain security firms have received notification and are involved in the response. The notice does not name an attacker or explain whether compromised keys, a signing process, software or another point of access enabled the transfers.

These are disclosures from the exchange's chief executive. They give customers an account of the response, but a technical report is still needed to evaluate the claimed containment and the cause of the incident.

## Withdrawals are paused; deposits and trading continue

The immediate operational change is the withdrawal suspension. Bitget describes it as a precaution pending a security review. Chen says account balances remain accurate, assets are protected, and deposits and trading continue to operate.

| Service or issue | Status in the CEO's notice |
|---|---|
| Withdrawals | Temporarily suspended pending security review |
| Deposits | Operational, according to Bitget |
| Trading | Operational, according to Bitget |
| Account balances | Accurate, according to Bitget |
| Cold wallets | Secure, according to Bitget |
| Withdrawal restart time | No specific time announced |
| Attack method | Investigation ongoing; no conclusion disclosed |

An accurate account balance and the ability to move assets off an exchange are separate matters. Customers may still see their balances and use trading functions during a withdrawal pause. That does not mean external transfers have resumed.

The CEO's promise of a report within 24 hours should also not be read as a withdrawal deadline. Her notice ties withdrawals to completion of the security review. It gives no fixed restart time.

## Why early loss estimates were lower

Before the CEO's statement, [Hacken](https://x.com/hackenclub/status/2103216092583964978) described a reported incident involving around $100 million and several assets, including USDT, ETH, BTC, AVAX and BNB. The firm said its investigation was ongoing. [PeckShield](https://x.com/peckshield/status/2103220911075631533) subsequently flagged a potential hot-wallet hack involving more than $178 million and linked an Ethereum address for inspection.

Those were preliminary observations from security researchers, rather than a final accounting from Bitget. [Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/bitget-hit-by-351m-hack-withdrawals-frozen-as-probe-begins/) also tracked the changing estimates before reporting the CEO's approximately $351.6 million assessment.

The figures should not be added together. They refer to evolving assessments of the same incident, with different information available to each observer. The latest company estimate is the appropriate figure to attribute to Bitget; it remains an estimate.

Wallet labels need similar care. An address label helps researchers identify a possible relationship with an exchange. It does not, by itself, reveal who authorized a particular transfer or whether a movement represents theft, internal rebalancing or an emergency transfer. The CEO's later statement that cold wallets remain secure differs from the implication of some early alerts. A reconciliation of affected addresses and transactions would help resolve that discrepancy.

## What the protection-fund statement establishes

Chen says the full estimated loss falls within the coverage of Bitget's User Protection Fund. The stated fund value of more than $464 million exceeds the company's approximately $351.6 million estimate of affected funds.

That comparison supports the arithmetic of the CEO's assurance. It is not an independent audit of the fund, an explanation of its available liquidity, or evidence that all incident-related transfers have already been reconciled. The notice does not describe the process or timing of any fund deployment.

For customers, the relevant follow-up is how Bitget applies that protection while restoring withdrawals. For investigators, the questions include the final amount transferred without authorization, any recoveries, the assets involved and whether all affected systems have been identified. A changing dollar estimate could reflect additional transactions, revised valuations or recovered assets; the present notice does not provide that breakdown.

## What remains unknown

The source of the compromise is still undisclosed. Chen wrote: “We will not speculate on the attack vector until the investigation is complete.” The statement does not support assigning responsibility to a particular hacking group, employee, vendor or software flaw.

It also does not provide a transaction-by-transaction loss schedule, a public recovery total or a detailed account of the controls that failed. Those gaps limit what can responsibly be concluded from the first disclosure. General explanations of exchange-wallet security should not be mistaken for evidence about this specific attack.

Chen promised hourly updates through her account and Bitget's official channels, followed by a full incident report covering root-cause analysis and corrective actions within 24 hours. Both are commitments to future disclosure, rather than completed findings.

The next useful update should answer two concrete questions: what authorized the transfers, and what has changed to make withdrawals safe to reopen?
