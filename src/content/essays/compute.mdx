Six countries or blocs have national AI compute programmes large enough to compare. The US donated access. The UK bought a machine. The EU built a federation. India rented. France counts private pledges. Japan runs through one company. I compared what each one publishes.

## The US

The United States did not buy a single GPU for its national programme. The National AI Research Resource pilot, launched in January 2024 under the National Science Foundation, coordinates donated compute from private companies and federal agencies and routes researchers and students to the machines.

The NSF NAIRR page, [last updated in March 2026](https://www.nsf.gov/focus-areas/artificial-intelligence/nairr), estimates ~$100 million in private-sector in-kind contributions over the pilot's first two years. The largest contributions: NVIDIA ($30 million in compute and software), Microsoft ($20 million in Azure credits), OpenAI ($1 million in model access), and Cerebras (up to four EXAFLOPs of compute). [28 nongovernmental partners](https://www.nsf.gov/focus-areas/artificial-intelligence/nairr) have contributed.

Fourteen federal agencies participate, from NASA and DARPA to the FDA and the Department of War. The Department of Energy and the National Institutes of Health co-lead a separate secure allocation track called NAIRR Secure for privacy-sensitive AI research.

The NAIRR pilot publishes a running tally on its portal, [nairrpilot.org](https://nairrpilot.org). As of August 2026: **840 research projects, 85 NAIRR Classroom awards, 23 infrastructure and data demonstration projects, 3 community workshops**. The NSF page, last updated March 2026, cited more than 600 projects and 6,000 students across all 50 states, Washington, DC, and Puerto Rico.

Washington did not buy GPUs and did not build halls. It routes people to donated machines and counts projects.

## The UK

The UK's AI Research Resource was announced in March 2023 with £900 million in funding. The first machine, Isambard-AI at the University of Bristol, was built with 5,000 NVIDIA Grace Hopper GH200 superchips at a cost of about £225 million. A second planned site, expected to be located in Cambridge or Edinburgh, did not proceed. The government was reported in early 2025 to have scrapped the second machine as part of budget cuts.

Nobody published a single account that ties the £900 million announcement to the one machine that got built. The gap stays open.

The UK now participates in the EU's AI Factory network as an antenna partner, listed as UKAIFA in the [European Commission's AI Factories catalogue](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-factories).

Britain bought one machine through public procurement and never published how much it was used.

## The EU

The EU's AI Factories publish more selection detail than any other programme. The [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-factories) publishes the full selection history.

The network now stands at 19 AI Factories and 13 antennas across the EU and partner countries. The Commission says at least nine new AI-optimised supercomputers will be procured, which it says will more than triple the EuroHPC AI computing capacity. Total investment through the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking for the 2021-2027 period is €10 billion, counting EU, member state, and associated country money.

The EU has also opened a call for up to seven AI Gigafactories, facilities of more than 100,000 advanced AI processors each, supported by up to €10 billion in public funding and an estimated €20 billion in private investment.

The EU publishes factory selections, site counts, and partner lists. The [EuroHPC AI Factories page](https://eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/ai-factories_en) says the factories offer free, customised support to SMEs and startups. It does not publish running hours delivered to those startups in a comparable format. Factory count is a commitment number. Hours delivered would be a delivery number. The second one is missing.

## India

India's programme is covered fully in [the IndiaAI essay](/indiaai). The model is empanelment rental: IndiaAI does not buy machines. It empanels private cloud providers, approves users, and subsidises access. MeitY reported 38,000 GPUs onboarded by February 2026. Cash released by that date: ₹400.94 crore, 3.9% of the ₹10,371.92 crore outlay. Press reporting put utilised GPUs at 7,418 of 33,099 committed. Both figures are documented in [the IndiaAI parliamentary record](/indiaai).

India's primary published metric is GPUs onboarded. Utilisation was reported by a newspaper, not by the programme.

## France

France treated private pledges as the national compute plan. At the AI Action Summit in Paris on 10-11 February 2025, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/details-110-billion-euros-investment-pledges-frances-ai-summit-2025-02-10/) Macron announcing about €109 billion in private AI investment in France. Brookfield was down for €20 billion. UAE financing, Macron's office said, could hit €50 billion. Those are pledges, not cash received.

The public side is the EU factories. The first French AI Factory, AI2F, was selected in the second wave in March 2025, tied to the Jean Zay supercomputer at GENCI. A second French factory was announced in the third wave in October 2025. France also hosts part of the MGX- and G42-backed data centre plans in [the debt essay](/aidebt), pledged at up to $52 billion.

France counts those pledges as national capacity. Hours would show whether any of it is running.

## Japan

Japan's programme is not a single named mission but a series of subsidies and joint ventures anchored by SoftBank, the parent company of ARM. The government has budgeted over ¥100 billion for AI compute subsidies through 2026, and the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry has backed domestic AI infrastructure through state-backed lenders.

SoftBank is the chairman and largest equity holder of Stargate, covered in [the debt essay](/aidebt). SoftBank's announced $19 billion equity commitment is largely funded through bank borrowing from Mizuho and other Japanese lenders. Japan's national AI compute is, in practice, SoftBank's balance sheet.

## What each programme counts

<img src="/images/essays/compute-table.svg" alt="Comparison table: six national AI compute programmes, their models, their primary metrics, and whether they publish utilisation hours. None publishes hours." class="w-full my-8" />

No programme publishes hours of GPU use per unit of public money. The US publishes projects supported. The EU publishes sites selected. India publishes GPUs onboarded. France counts private pledges. Japan counts SoftBank's balance sheet. The UK published an announcement and then stopped.

They count what their model makes easy to count. The US never bought GPUs, so it counts projects. India rents from empanelled clouds, so it counts what those clouds report onboarded. Utilisation sat in a letter, not a dashboard. France counts summit pledges. Japan counts SoftBank. None of them publishes hours.
