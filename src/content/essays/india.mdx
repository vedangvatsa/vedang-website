The best-known official ranking of Indian cities is already six years old. The latest monthly price data is current to July 2026. Put those facts next to each other and the problem becomes clear: India has recent data about prices, but no recent national score that measures what it feels like to live in each city.

## What this ranking can and cannot say

The [Ease of Living dashboard](https://eol.smartcities.gov.in/dashboard) from the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs gives us the best common starting point. The 2020 assessment covers 111 cities. It uses 49 indicators in 14 categories. The categories include education, health, housing, water and sanitation, mobility, safety, recreation, economic opportunity, environment and resilience.

The assessment gives 35% of the final score to Quality of Life, 15% to Economic Ability, 20% to Sustainability and 30% to the Citizen Perception Survey. The citizen survey gathered more than 32 lakh responses through online and face-to-face channels. This gives residents a large voice in the score, but it also makes the date of the survey matter. The responses came in February and March 2020, before the pandemic changed work, travel, school and public health.

The index ranks cities as they looked in that assessment. It does not tell us what rent costs in 2026. It does not measure the current air in a city. It does not tell a renter how much money remains after housing. A careful reading therefore needs two layers:

1. The official city score, which lets us compare the same framework across cities.
2. Newer official measures that tell us how prices, household spending and public programmes have changed since that score.

The result is a dated but transparent scorecard. It is more useful than pretending that one number captures a whole city.

## The official ranking

The top of the Million+ list has a clear shape. [The MoHUA report](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf) gives Bengaluru 66.70, Pune 66.27 and Ahmedabad 64.87. Chennai, Surat and Navi Mumbai followed. The gap between the first and the sixth city is five points on a 100-point scale. That is close enough that small changes in the framework or data year could alter the order.

The ranking also contains a warning. Greater Mumbai placed tenth at 58.23. Hyderabad placed twenty-fourth at 55.40. Delhi placed thirteenth at 57.56. These are not simple judgments about whether residents prefer one city to another. The scores reflect the inputs, city submissions, public data, the index weights and citizen answers.

<IndiaCityRankings />

The list above includes only Million+ cities. The [government report](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf) keeps cities below one million people in a separate group because comparing a large metropolitan area with a smaller city creates a population problem. Shimla leads that second group at 60.90, followed by Bhubaneswar at 59.85. That result matters because smaller cities often disappear when a single national table favours scale.

The report also says that some data points had to use district or state geography when city-level data did not exist. That is a useful admission. It means the word “city” in an official ranking can sometimes describe a wider administrative area. A reader should not treat every decimal place as a measurement of one municipal boundary.

## Bengaluru wins for a specific reason

Bengaluru tops the overall Million+ ranking, but the score does not come from one uniform advantage. The [MoHUA city table](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf) gives it an Economic Ability score of 78.82, far above most other large cities. Its Quality of Life score is 55.67, which sits below Chennai, Coimbatore, Indore, Pune, Surat and several others.

The city’s result shows why an overall rank can mislead. Bengaluru has a large economic advantage inside this framework. It does not lead every service category. A person choosing a city for work may value the economic score more than a person choosing for air, schools, water or daily travel.

Pune gives a different answer. Its Sustainability score is 75.74, the highest among the cities in the selected pillar comparison. [The same MoHUA table](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf) gives Chennai the highest Quality of Life score among Million+ cities at 60.84 and a Citizen Perception score of 82.60. The same city can therefore look different depending on which question we ask.

<IndiaCityPillars />

The three pillars explain more than the final order:

- Bengaluru wins the economic measure, but its quality score is ordinary among the leaders.
- Pune combines a high quality score with the highest sustainability score in this group.
- Chennai leads the quality score and records the highest citizen perception score in the group.
- Ahmedabad scores well across all three administrative pillars.
- Indore has a high quality score and a good sustainability score, but a much lower economic score.
- Mumbai has a reasonable sustainability score but a lower quality score than the leading cities.
- Hyderabad has a middling quality score, economic score and sustainability score, which leaves it below the leaders.

This is a better way to read a city ranking: ask which component produced the result, then ask whether that component matches the life you want.

## Quality of life is not the same as income

The [MoHUA report](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf) defines seven Quality of Life categories: education, health, housing and shelter, water and waste services, mobility, safety and recreation. The national average for this pillar was 51.38. Safety and security scored 86.7 on average, housing and shelter scored 79.5, and education scored 70.7. Recreation was the weakest category.

These numbers describe the index, not a universal truth about daily life. A high safety score can coexist with under-reporting of crime. A high housing score can coexist with expensive private rent. A high mobility score can hide a long commute for people who live far from the main transit routes.

The report’s own category details help explain the uneven results. Only 36.9% of participating cities had more than 90% of households with piped water. Some large cities had a significant share of residents in slums. Many cities had weak sewerage coverage. These are not small details. They affect health, time, household work and the cost of living.

The city score also mixes outcomes with administrative reporting. The government framework uses percentages and ratios where possible, such as sewer connections per household or transport fatalities per lakh people. That improves comparison, but it cannot remove every difference in definitions, reporting quality and city boundary.

The latest official sources can update individual pieces:

- The [National Health Mission quarterly MIS report](https://nhm.gov.in/index4.php?lang=1&level=0&lid=686&linkid=457) publishes health programme status up to 31 December 2025.
- The [UDISE+ publications](https://dsel.education.gov.in/en/statistics) provide school data for 2024-25, with state and district reporting.
- The [NCRB Crime in India 2023 catalogue](https://www.data.gov.in/catalog/crime-india-2023) includes city-level crime tables for metropolitan cities.
- The [Smart Cities open data portal](https://smartcities.data.gov.in/) contains thousands of datasets from city administrations, including transport, health, traffic and civic services.

Those sources can update the story, but they do not yet form one single, current, government-issued quality-of-life score for every city in the country.

## The cost of living problem

Government data does not provide a current, complete table of rent, food, transport, utilities and salaries for every Indian city. That prevents a responsible city-by-city cost-of-living rank using official sources alone.

The closest consistent measure is Monthly Per Capita Consumption Expenditure, or MPCE, from the [Household Consumption Expenditure Survey 2023-24](https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/HCES%20FactSheet%202023-24.pdf). MoSPI estimated average urban MPCE at ₹6,996. The figure covers what households spend per person. It is not a rent quote and it is not a salary measure.

The chart maps each selected city to its state or union territory average. A high number can mean that households spend more because prices are higher, because incomes are higher, because households buy more services, or because all three occur together. A low number does not prove that a city is cheap for a renter.

<IndiaAffordabilityProxy />

The [HCES state table](https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/HCES%20FactSheet%202023-24.pdf) still shows useful differences. Urban MPCE was ₹8,978 in Telangana, ₹8,534 in Delhi, ₹8,165 in Tamil Nadu, ₹8,076 in Karnataka and ₹7,363 in Maharashtra. It was ₹5,538 in Madhya Pradesh and ₹5,395 in Uttar Pradesh. These figures tell us where average household spending sits. They do not tell us the monthly cost of a one-bedroom apartment in Hyderabad or Bengaluru.

The [HCES expenditure table](https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/HCES%20FactSheet%202023-24.pdf) also puts urban rent at 6.56% of MPCE in its expenditure shares. That figure includes the survey’s treatment of households that own their homes. It should not become a claim that a typical renter spends only 6.56% of income on housing. A renter in a high-demand neighbourhood can face a much larger share.

MoSPI’s new CPI series gives us a current price clock. The [July 2026 release](https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/latestReleases/latest_release_1786529680747_3113661d-1a2b-4b9a-af06-b340193ef9a0_Press_Release_CPI_July_2026.pdf) uses 2024 as its base year and covers rural and urban markets across India. Urban CPI inflation reached 3.96% year on year in July. Urban housing inflation reached 2.01%. The CPI housing measure combines actual rental payments with maintenance, repair, security and other dwelling services. It measures price change, not the level of rent in each city.

<IndiaPricePulse />

The two lines in the [MoSPI release](https://www.mospi.gov.in/uploads/latestReleases/latest_release_1786529680747_3113661d-1a2b-4b9a-af06-b340193ef9a0_Press_Release_CPI_July_2026.pdf) tell us something useful. General urban inflation rose from 2.75% in January to 3.96% in July. Housing inflation stayed near 2%. Food, transport and other items therefore carried more of the recent price pressure than the official housing index. That does not make rent unimportant. Rent remains a large monthly bill even when its year-on-year index rises slowly.

The [Department of Consumer Affairs Price Monitoring Division](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/price-monitoring-cell/price-monitoring-cell) collects daily prices for essential commodities from 550 market centres. That source can support food-price comparisons. Its reporting centres do not cover every city, the product quality can vary by centre, and the public system does not provide a complete household basket for each city. Those limits matter when someone asks for a precise city cost ranking.

## Air changes the answer

Air quality deserves separate treatment because it affects both health and the practical value of a cheap city. The official CPCB system has several different measures:

- The [National Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Programme](https://cpcb.nic.in/namp-data/) provides year-wise monitoring data.
- The [National Air Quality Index](https://airquality.cpcb.gov.in/AQI_India/) provides current station and city readings.
- The [PRANA portal](https://prana.cpcb.gov.in/) tracks the National Clean Air Programme across 130 cities.
- The [Swachh Vayu Survekshan 2024 results](https://prana.cpcb.gov.in/ncapServices/robust/fetchFilesFromDrive/Swachh_Vayu_Survekshan_2024_Result.pdf) rank city action and PM10 improvement.

The last measure is easy to misuse. The [CPCB results](https://prana.cpcb.gov.in/ncapServices/robust/fetchFilesFromDrive/Swachh_Vayu_Survekshan_2024_Result.pdf) place Surat, Jabalpur and Agra in the top three places in the Million+ category in the 2024 Swachh Vayu Survekshan. That result measures the programme’s assessment of city action and PM10 improvement. It is not a simple league table of annual PM2.5 exposure.

<IndiaAirActionChart />

The distinction changes how a ranking should work. A city can improve its official action score while residents still face bad air in one season or neighbourhood. A single annual average can also hide the difference between a clean coastal area and a polluted traffic corridor. The government’s station data is the right place to study that detail, but the data needs consistent coverage and a fixed annual window before it enters a composite score.

## Why a single final rank would be false precision

A composite rank looks clean because it compresses many facts into one order. The compression also hides choices.

Suppose we gave air quality a larger weight. Pune, Visakhapatnam or a city with better environmental data could rise. Suppose we gave current household spending a larger weight. Indore or Lucknow could look more attractive than Mumbai. Suppose we gave economic opportunity 40% instead of 15%. Bengaluru and Delhi would gain. Suppose we cared most about current prices for a family with children. The answer would depend on schools, rent, health care and transport, not the official 2020 score.

The Ease of Living Index already makes one set of choices. It gives citizen perception 30% and economic ability 15%. That may be a reasonable public-policy design, but it is not a law of city quality. A private employee, a student, a family with a newborn, a person with a disability and a retiree can use different weights and reach different results.

This is why this essay does not create a fake 2026 “best city” rank by combining incomparable values. It shows the official rank, the pillar scores, the spending proxy and the latest price trend separately. The reader can see where the evidence is solid and where the government does not publish enough detail.

<Callout type="insight" title="A rule for reading the charts">
  Treat the 2020 Ease of Living score as a common baseline. Treat MPCE as a state-level spending measure. Treat CPI as a current price-change measure. Treat air rankings as programme results. None of the four is a rent quote.
</Callout>

## A practical reading of the cities

### Bengaluru

Bengaluru is the official Million+ winner because its economic score is far ahead of the field. The city also has a respectable citizen perception score. Its quality-of-life score does not match its economic position. A worker who values access to jobs may accept congestion, water stress or housing costs that lower the service score. A family choosing schools, health access and daily travel may make a different choice.

### Pune

Pune has the second-highest overall score and the best sustainability score in the selected group. It also scores well on quality and economic ability. The result is more balanced than Bengaluru’s. That does not remove the need to check neighbourhood-level housing and commuting conditions, which the national index cannot show.

### Chennai

Chennai leads the Quality of Life pillar among Million+ cities and records the highest citizen perception score in this comparison, according to the [MoHUA report](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf). Its urban Tamil Nadu MPCE is ₹8,165, above the all-India average, according to [MoSPI](https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/HCES%20FactSheet%202023-24.pdf). The city therefore combines good official service scores with a state spending level that sits toward the upper end of the selected group.

### Ahmedabad and Surat

Ahmedabad places third overall and scores well in all three administrative pillars in the [MoHUA assessment](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf). Surat places fifth, with high quality, sustainability and citizen perception scores. Surat also won the Million+ category in the [2024 CPCB clean-air action assessment](https://prana.cpcb.gov.in/ncapServices/robust/fetchFilesFromDrive/Swachh_Vayu_Survekshan_2024_Result.pdf). These are two of the clearest cases where service results and air-policy results point in the same direction, although the dates and measures differ.

### Indore

Indore scores ninth overall and has one of the highest Quality of Life scores in the [official table](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf). Its urban Madhya Pradesh MPCE is ₹5,538, below the national urban average in [MoSPI’s HCES](https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/HCES%20FactSheet%202023-24.pdf). That can make the city look attractive to a household comparing everyday spending. The number still says nothing about a particular apartment, salary or commute.

### Mumbai and Delhi

Greater Mumbai and Delhi show why large economic centres do not automatically lead a quality-of-life table. The [MoHUA scores](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf) put Delhi’s Economic Ability score at 50.73 and its Quality of Life score at 51.22. Greater Mumbai’s overall score is 58.23, with a Quality of Life score of 51.12. High wages, large job markets and public services can coexist with high housing pressure, pollution, long travel and uneven access across neighbourhoods.

### Hyderabad

Hyderabad ranks twenty-fourth among the Million+ cities at 55.40 in the [MoHUA report](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf). Telangana records the highest urban MPCE among the large states shown here at ₹8,978 in [MoSPI’s HCES](https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/HCES%20FactSheet%202023-24.pdf). That pairing is a useful warning against calling a city affordable because its official Ease of Living rank sits below Bengaluru or Pune. Household spending and public-service scores answer different questions.

### Smaller cities

Shimla leads the official Less than Million group, followed by Bhubaneswar. Kochi ranks thirty-ninth in that group, and Thiruvananthapuram ranks twenty-first. Smaller cities can offer better scores in a category without matching the job depth or specialist health services of a large metro. The right comparison depends on what a person needs to do in the city.

## What a better government ranking would publish

India already has most of the pieces. The missing piece is a shared city-level release schedule and a common geography.

An annual public scorecard should publish:

1. A fixed list of city boundaries and the district or metropolitan area used for each measure.
2. A current quality-of-life score with the year of every input.
3. Median rent by dwelling size and distance from public transport, with a clear sample frame.
4. Food, fuel, electricity, water, education and health spending from the same household survey.
5. Travel time and transit access by ward, not only a city average.
6. Annual PM2.5 and PM10 exposure with station coverage and missing-data rules.
7. Health facilities, school quality and crime rates with population denominators.
8. Income and employment data so high spending is not confused with high cost.
9. Separate scores for families, students, retirees and lower-income households.
10. A sensitivity chart showing how the order changes when the weights change.

The government has already built useful pieces of this system. MoHUA has the Ease of Living framework and the Smart Cities data portal. MoSPI has HCES and the revised CPI. CPCB has the air monitoring and PRANA systems. The National Crime Records Bureau has city crime data. The National Health Mission and Ministry of Education publish current programme and school data. A public annual score would need these systems to agree on city boundaries, dates and definitions.

## The ranking

For the evidence available today, the most defensible answer is:

- **Best official Million+ score:** [Bengaluru, 66.70](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf).
- **Best official Quality of Life score among Million+ cities:** [Chennai, 60.84](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf).
- **Best official Sustainability score among the selected Million+ cities:** [Pune, 75.74](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf).
- **Best official Economic Ability score:** [Bengaluru, 78.82](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf).
- **Best official Citizen Perception score among the selected cities:** [Chennai, 82.60](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf).
- **Best official Less than Million score:** [Shimla, 60.90](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/final_web_ease_of_living_report_2020_.pdf).
- **Best 2024 Million+ clean-air action result:** [Surat](https://prana.cpcb.gov.in/ncapServices/robust/fetchFilesFromDrive/Swachh_Vayu_Survekshan_2024_Result.pdf).

These are seven different answers because city life contains seven different questions.

The honest government-only conclusion is therefore narrower than a typical listicle. Bengaluru leads the old overall index. Chennai leads the quality and citizen-perception measures in this comparison. Pune leads sustainability. Surat leads the 2024 clean-air action assessment. Indore combines a high quality score with lower state-level urban spending. Hyderabad shows why spending and service scores should not merge without care.

The next national Ease of Living release will matter more than another opinion poll. If MoHUA publishes one with current city data, it should be read alongside the July 2026 CPI, the 2023-24 household survey and the latest CPCB station records. Until then, the useful question is not “Which city is number one?” It is “Which city performs well on the things that make up my life, and how recent is the evidence?”
