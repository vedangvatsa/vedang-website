The Union Cabinet approved **₹10,371.92 crore** for the IndiaAI Mission in March 2024. By 9 February 2026, IndiaAI, the MeitY implementation arm, had received **₹400.94 crore**. That is **3.9%** of the outlay, 23 months into a 60-month plan. The figure comes from a [Rajya Sabha annex](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf), Unstarred Question 1668, answered on 13 February 2026.

The same reply said more than **38,000 GPUs** had been "onboarded."

## Where did the money go?

Mission money sits in four separate ledgers. Public statements mix them up.

1. **Outlay.** The Cabinet ceiling of ₹10,371.92 crore, fixed in March 2024.
2. **Estimate.** The annual Budget Estimates and Revised Estimates that MeitY actually carries.
3. **Release.** Cash that moves from the Ministry of Finance to IndiaAI, disclosed in parliamentary replies.
4. **Spend.** What IndiaAI then pays to providers, startups, and institutions. This ledger is not published pillar-wise.

The [13 February 2026 annex](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf) publishes estimates and releases for IndiaAI as a whole, and outlay pillar-wise. It does not publish spend at all.

The table below uses two abbreviations. BE is the Budget Estimate, what MeitY asked for at the start of the year. RE is the Revised Estimate, the figure after the mid-year cut.

| Year | BE (₹ Cr) | RE (₹ Cr) | Received (₹ Cr) | Share of RE | Share of BE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2024-25 | 551.75 | 173.00 | 21.79 | 12.6% | 3.9% |
| 2025-26 | 2,000.00 | 800.00 | 379.15 | 47.4% | 19.0% |
| 2026-27 | 1,000.00 | n/a | 0.00 | 0% | 0% |
| **Total** | n/a | n/a | **400.94** | n/a | **3.9% of outlay** |

The release and estimate figures come from the [Rajya Sabha reply of 13 February 2026](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf), with cash recorded to 9 February 2026. The ₹2,000 crore Budget Estimate for 2025-26 comes from the [Union Budget documents](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_at_Glance/budget_at_a_glance.pdf). [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-indiaai-mission-allocation-reduced-69-percent-2024-2025-estimates/) covered the FY 2024-25 estimate cut in February 2025 and [the same cash figures](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-indiaai-mission-400-crore-over-rs-10000-crore-5-year-outlay-released/) in April 2026.

The annual ask has been cut every year since approval. FY 2024-25 was trimmed **69%**, from [₹551.75 crore to ₹173 crore](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-indiaai-mission-allocation-reduced-69-percent-2024-2025-estimates/). FY 2025-26 was trimmed **60%**, from ₹2,000 crore to ₹800 crore. FY 2026-27 asks for ₹1,000 crore, half of the year before.

Releases ran even further behind. Year one delivered 12.6% of the revised estimate, and 3.9% of what MeitY originally asked for. Year two delivered 47.4% of the revised estimate, and 19% of the ask.

The five-year ceiling was never meant to be released in one year. But the releases lagged even the reduced estimates in both years. [MediaNama published the same cash reading](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-indiaai-mission-400-crore-over-rs-10000-crore-5-year-outlay-released/) in April 2026.

A note on FY 2026-27. The reply was cut off on 9 February 2026, before the first release of the new financial year. The zero in the Received column reflects the cut-off date, not the full year.

## What does "38,000 GPUs" mean?

The GPU count needs checking too. Officials talked about GPU numbers. The cash shown to Parliament was much smaller. The claims below come from PIB, the government's Press Information Bureau, and from MeitY's reply to Parliament. The dated claims and the cash record follow.

| Actor | Date | Claim | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cabinet / PIB | Mar 2024 | ₹10,300 Cr+ outlay. 10,000+ GPUs via public-private partnership. | [PIB 2012375](https://pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2012375) |
| PIB | 30 May 2025 | 34,333 GPUs empaneled (18,417 + 15,916). | [PIB 2132817](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817) |
| PIB | 12 Oct 2025 | 38,000+ GPUs onboarded. ₹65 per hour. | [PIB 2178092](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092) |
| MeitY | 13 Feb 2026 | ₹21.79 Cr then ₹379.15 Cr received. | [Q.1668](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf) |
| MeitY | 13 Feb 2026 | 38,000+ GPUs. 14 providers empaneled. | [Q.1668](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf) |
| MeitY | 13 Feb 2026 | 114 researchers, 47 startups, 58 govt. | [Q.1668](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf) |

The GPU number grew from 10,000 in the March 2024 Cabinet note to 34,333 empaneled in May 2025 to 38,000+ onboarded in October 2025. Cash received went from ₹21.79 crore to ₹379.15 crore in the same period.

The 38,000 figure breaks down further. IndiaAI does not buy and store chips. It empanels cloud providers, approves users, and subsidises access. The [30 May 2025 release from PIB, the government's Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817), published the lowest bid, or L1, prices from seven bidders, including Cyfuture, Netmagic, Sify, Locuz, Vensysco, Ishan Infotech, and Yotta.

| Model | On-demand (₹/hr) | 1-month (₹/hr) | L1 (lowest bid) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NVIDIA L4 (1X) | 45.07 | 29.00 | Sify / Netmagic |
| Intel Gaudi 2 (1X) | 57.60 | 46.80 | CyFuture |
| NVIDIA L40S (1X) | 67.50 | 49.50 | CyFuture |
| NVIDIA A100 80GB | 135.90 | 89.10 | CyFuture |
| NVIDIA H200 SXM | 140.00 | 135.00 | Netmagic |
| NVIDIA H100 SXM | 153.00 | 134.10 | CyFuture |
| NVIDIA B200 SXM | 323.00 | 308.00 | Yotta |

Source [PIB Press Release ID 2132817](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817) (30 May 2025), Table 1.

The ₹65/hour figure in the [October 2025 PIB explainer](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092) applies to entry-level GPUs on one-month pricing. An H100 SXM is **₹153/hour** on-demand. A B200 SXM is **₹323/hour**. The February 2025 tender round averaged [₹115.85 per GPU-hour](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-indiaai-mission-allocation-reduced-69-percent-2024-2025-estimates/).

Committed GPUs pass through several stages before anyone uses them. [The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/underused-gpus-raise-questions-about-indiaai-capacity-build-out/articleshow/128981840.cms), citing an IndiaAI letter to providers, reported these numbers.

| Stage | GPUs | Share of committed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Committed | 33,099 | 100% |
| Assigned | 12,638 | 38% |
| Utilised by end users | 7,418 | 22% |

These figures come from the [Economic Times report](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/underused-gpus-raise-questions-about-indiaai-capacity-build-out/articleshow/128981840.cms), which cites an IndiaAI letter to empanelled providers. Treat them as press-reported until MeitY publishes the same series itself.

Two of every three committed GPUs had not been assigned when the ET report was filed. Roughly one in five was in use. The government then announced the next target, a [second tender round drawing bids for 18,000 GPUs](https://www.medianama.com/2025/05/223-indiaai-mission-18000-gpu-bids-indigenous-ai-models/), before it published utilisation of the first. IndiaAI CEO Abhishek Singh said in May 2025 that about 15,000 of the bids would clear technical checks.

The same report noted who the bidders partner with. Vensysco and Locuz with AWS. Ishan Infotech with Oracle. Netmagic with Google Cloud. IndiaAI's national compute is mostly rented from big cloud companies through Indian resellers. The Mission pays for access to machines it does not own. That is a deliberate design choice. It also means the GPU count refers to machines owned by private companies.

## Who is actually using the machines?

The reply to Parliament also included user counts.

| Group | Slots | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Researchers | 114 | 37% |
| Government | 58 | 19% |
| Startups & MSMEs | 47 | 15% |
| Early startups | 36 | 12% |
| Students | 32 | 10% |
| Early researchers | 10 | 3% |
| IndiaAI Fellows | 8 | 3% |

Source [Rajya Sabha Unstarred Q.1668](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf). Rows may overlap; listed slots sum to 305.

305 slots. 33,099 committed GPUs. More than one hundred GPUs for every named user slot. Nobody publishes the two numbers on the same page, because the page would not read as a success story.

The single largest category is academic researchers, 114 of them. IndiaAI Fellows, the programme's own flagship cohort, number eight. [Takshashila warned in April 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/04/223-indiaai-mission-compute-capacity-underused-takshashila-report/) that strict eligibility rules and slow processes would leave Mission compute underused, and ranked India last among the US, China, and the EU for AI governance transparency. The February 2026 parliamentary reply and the 2026 utilisation figures point the same way.

## What did the government get for the Sarvam money?

The largest single deal in the Mission's first two years is Sarvam. On 26 April 2025, [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817) said Sarvam AI was selected to build a sovereign open-source 120B-parameter stack. Twelve teams had been shortlisted for foundation models by the [February 2026 Rajya Sabha reply](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf). In April 2026, [BharatGen was reported with over ₹1,000 crore](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-centre-funds-12-ai-projects-sovereign-models-bharatgen-4x-next-highest-allocation-rs-1000-crore/), four times the next-highest allocation among the twelve.

Sarvam received the largest compute subsidy in the programme. [MediaNama, citing Economic Times](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-govt-minority-stake-sarvam-indiaai-mission/), reported the terms.

| Term | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Compute subsidy | ₹98.68 Cr |
| Total compute bill | ₹246.71 Cr |
| GPUs | 4,096 × NVIDIA H100 |
| Duration | 6 months |
| Instrument | Compulsorily convertible debentures |
| Potential conversion | 1-2% government stake |
| Sarvam Series B first close | $234M (June 2026) |
| Post-money valuation | ~$1.5B |

Press-reported via [Economic Times, as covered by MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-govt-minority-stake-sarvam-indiaai-mission/). The commercial terms are not in the Rajya Sabha PDF.

The subsidy is 40% of the bill, matching the subsidy level MeitY announced in [January 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-indiaai-mission-allocation-reduced-69-percent-2024-2025-estimates/). The support comes as compulsorily convertible debentures. If the CCDs convert, the government ends up with a 1-2% ownership stake in Sarvam.

Sarvam closed a $234 million round in June 2026, days before [US export controls limited access to Anthropic's frontier models](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-anthropic-fable-5-mythos-5-suspension-us-export-controls/). Some IndiaAI participants have said they do not want to give up equity in exchange for compute support. The Sarvam terms, reported in June 2026, are the first public case of that trade-off.

In January 2026, Tamil Nadu signed a [₹10,000 crore MoU with Sarvam](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-tamil-nadu-sarvam-ai-sovereign-ai-park/) for a sovereign AI park, roughly the size of the entire central Mission. As of mid-2026, public AI infrastructure in India runs on at least three money flows. The central Mission. The state MoU. Private funding rounds.

## Is the Mission failing?

It is too early to call the Mission a failure. Renting rather than buying GPUs is a deliberate design choice. When utilisation lags, providers sit on idle machines while the government reports a GPU count. Both sides can announce the next capacity target before publishing how much the last one was used.

Private money dwarfs the Mission. Amazon and Microsoft have committed [over $50 billion to India's cloud and AI infrastructure](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-amazon-microsoft-52b-india-ai-investment/), far more than IndiaAI has received. That is company money, separate from the Mission total. Who owns the machines matters, because the ₹10,372 crore Mission pays for access, and the machines stay with the companies.

The scoreboard is where the Mission falls short. India's most successful digital programmes counted completed transactions, UPI payments and Aadhaar authentications, as argued in the [API States](/apis) and [bureaucracy](/bureaucracy) essays. IndiaAI counts onboarded GPUs and Cabinet outlays. The programmes that worked reported transactions. IndiaAI reports counts.

## What would prove the Mission is working?

Three things would tell us whether the Mission is working.

1. **Release cadence.** Quarterly releases from MeitY to IndiaAI, published against the annual estimate.
2. **Hours used per rupee.** Utilisation in GPU-hours, not counts, published next to subsidy rupees.
3. **CCD terms.** The Sarvam conversion price, licence terms, and any converted equity, in a primary document.

The pillar-wise outlay table exists. The pillar-wise spend table does not.

By early 2026, IndiaAI had received ₹401 crore of a ₹10,372 crore Cabinet outlay, while public messaging led with 38,000+ onboarded GPUs. The estimates shrank every year, the releases ran behind the estimates, and the utilisation ran behind the releases.

The Mission can still ship models, labs, and subsidised hours. The test is whether IndiaAI reports hours used per rupee as often as it reports GPU counts.
