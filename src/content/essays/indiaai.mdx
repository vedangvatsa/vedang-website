The Union Cabinet approved ₹10,371.92 crore for the IndiaAI Mission in [March 2024](https://pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2012375). By 9 February 2026, IndiaAI, the MeitY implementation arm, had received ₹400.94 crore. That is 3.9% of the outlay, 23 months into a 60-month plan. The cash figure comes from a [Rajya Sabha annex](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf), Unstarred Question 1668, answered on 13 February 2026.

The same reply said more than 38,000 GPUs had been "onboarded." Empaneling GPUs and releasing money are different acts. One is a list of machines that providers say they can offer. The other is cash leaving the Ministry of Finance.

## How to read Mission money

Mission money sits in 4 ledgers. Public statements often mix them.

1. **Outlay.** The Cabinet ceiling of ₹10,371.92 crore, fixed in March 2024. This is the 5-year outer limit. It is not a promise that cash will arrive in year 1.
2. **Estimate.** The annual Budget Estimate and Revised Estimate that MeitY asks for. The Budget Estimate is the ask at the start of the year. The Revised Estimate is the mid-year cut.
3. **Release.** Cash that moves from the Ministry of Finance to IndiaAI. Parliament has published this for IndiaAI as a whole.
4. **Spend.** What IndiaAI then pays to providers and institutions. Pillar-wise spend has not been published in the same annex that lists pillar-wise outlay.

The 13 February 2026 annex publishes estimates and releases for IndiaAI as a whole, and outlay pillar-wise. It does not publish spend. Without spend, a reader can see the ceiling and the cash in, but not how the cash was split across compute, models, skills, datasets, and safety.

The outlay split is in the annex and in [MeitY's earlier Lok Sabha disclosure](https://www.medianama.com/2024/12/223-indiaai-mission-expenditure-budget/).

<Figure src="/images/essays/indiaai-pillars.svg?v=1" alt="IndiaAI Cabinet outlay by pillar. Compute Capacity 4,563.36 crore (44%). Safe and Trusted AI 20.46 crore (0.2%)." source="Rajya Sabha Q.1668" sourceUrl="https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf" wide />

Safe & Trusted AI gets about 1/5 of the overhead line. The sub-project breakdown inside Compute Capacity's ₹4,563.36 crore has never been published, as [MediaNama noted](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-indiaai-mission-allocation-reduced-69-percent-2024-2025-estimates/) in February 2025. Compute is the largest pillar and the least transparent inside the pillar.

Start with the design as written. IndiaAI was never a single cheque. It was a multi-year ceiling with annual asks that Finance could trim. Slow releases do not, by themselves, prove mismanagement. They do prove that the number most often repeated in public, ₹10,372 crore, is not the number that describes what IndiaAI could spend in its first 2 years.

## The cash, year by year

The estimates were cut every year. Then the releases ran further behind the cuts.

BE is the Budget Estimate, what MeitY asked for at the start of the year. RE is the Revised Estimate, the figure after the mid-year cut. Received is cash that reached IndiaAI.

<Figure src="/images/essays/indiaai-cascade.svg?v=4" alt="IndiaAI cash: year 1 asked 551.75 crore, received 21.79 crore. Year 2 asked 2,000 crore, received 379.15 crore." source="Rajya Sabha Q.1668" sourceUrl="https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf" wide />

[MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-indiaai-mission-allocation-reduced-69-percent-2024-2025-estimates/) covered the FY 2024-25 cut in February 2025 and [the same cash figures](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-indiaai-mission-400-crore-over-rs-10000-crore-5-year-outlay-released/) in April 2026.

Every annual ask was cut. FY 2024-25 was trimmed 69%, from ₹551.75 crore to ₹173 crore. FY 2025-26 was trimmed 60%, from ₹2,000 crore to ₹800 crore. FY 2026-27 asks for ₹1,000 crore, half of the previous year's ask.

Against the original Budget Estimate, FY 2024-25 delivered 3.9% of what MeitY asked for, and FY 2025-26 delivered 19%. The 5-year ceiling was never meant to be released in 1 year. The releases still lagged even the reduced estimates both years.

[MediaNama's February 2025 coverage](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-indiaai-mission-allocation-reduced-69-percent-2024-2025-estimates/) listed 3 possible reasons for the FY 2024-25 cut. GPU bids came in cheaper than expected. Rollout was phased. Money may have been moved to other priorities. Those reasons can be true at the same time. Cheaper bids should free capacity, not freeze releases. Phased rollout should produce a published schedule of releases against milestones. Reallocation should leave a paper trail in Budget documents. The public record shows the cuts. It does not fully settle which reason dominated.

A note on FY 2026-27. The reply was cut off on 9 February 2026, before the first release of the new financial year. The zero reflects the cut-off date, not the full year. Any update after that date needs a fresh parliamentary reply or Budget paper.

The GPU number grew from 10,000 in March 2024 to 34,333 empaneled in May 2025 to 38,000+ onboarded in October 2025. Cash received went from ₹21.79 crore to ₹379.15 crore in the same period. Both series can be true. Empanelment can grow while cash stays thin if the programme rents capacity on demand and pays only for approved use. The problem for public understanding is that "38,000 GPUs" sounds like a national fleet. In this design it is closer to a catalogue.

## How the compute model works

IndiaAI does not buy and store chips in a government warehouse. It empanels cloud providers, approves users, and subsidises access. The user gets a lower hourly rate. The provider gets paid for the hours used. The government does not own the machine when the hour ends.

The [30 May 2025 PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817) published the lowest bid (L1) prices from 7 bidders including Cyfuture, Netmagic, Sify, Locuz, Vensysco, Ishan Infotech, and Yotta.

The [October 2025 PIB explainer](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092) says GPUs are available at a subsidised rate of ₹65 per hour. In January 2025, MeitY had announced a 40% subsidy with a target of [₹100 per hour](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-indiaai-mission-allocation-reduced-69-percent-2024-2025-estimates/). The May 2025 tender table shows L1 prices from ₹29 to ₹323 per hour depending on the model and term. An H100 SXM is ₹153/hour on-demand. A B200 SXM is ₹323/hour. The February 2025 tender round averaged [₹115.85 per GPU-hour](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-indiaai-mission-allocation-reduced-69-percent-2024-2025-estimates/).

These numbers sit next to each other without a public reconciliation. ₹65 per hour is a headline subsidy rate. The L1 table is a bid sheet by model. A researcher using an L4 at the month rate pays far less than a lab using a B200 on demand. Averaging them into 1 number hides the product mix. A mature scorecard would publish hours by model class, not a single rupee-per-hour slogan.

GPUs pass through several stages before anyone uses them. [The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/underused-gpus-raise-questions-about-indiaai-capacity-build-out/articleshow/128981840.cms), citing an IndiaAI letter to providers, reported these numbers.

Press-reported. Not a parliamentary utilisation table.</p>

On those press-reported figures, 2 of every 3 committed GPUs had not been assigned, and roughly 1 in 5 was in use. That is the newspaper's arithmetic on the letter it cited, not a parliamentary utilisation table. The government then announced the next target, a second tender round. [Economic Times reported on 13 May 2025](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/govt-gets-bids-offering-18000-gpus-in-round-2-of-indiaai-mission-tender/articleshow/121118580.cms) that IndiaAI CEO Abhishek Singh said bids offered 18,000 GPUs, that about 15,000 would clear technical checks, that more than 30,000 GPU requests were in the pipeline, and that a third tender round was open. Those figures are Singh speaking to ET. They are not in a PIB release. The later official count is in the [30 May 2025 PIB note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817): 34,333 GPUs empaneled (18,417 + 15,916). The January 2025 target had been 18,693 GPUs, announced when the initial 10,000 figure was [expanded](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-indiaai-mission-allocation-reduced-69-percent-2024-2025-estimates/), before that May expansion.

[MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/05/223-indiaai-mission-18000-gpu-bids-indigenous-ai-models/), covering the same tender, named the bidders' partners. Vensysco and Locuz with AWS. Ishan Infotech with Oracle. Netmagic with Google Cloud. IndiaAI's national compute is mostly rented from big cloud companies through Indian resellers. The Mission pays for access to machines it does not own. The design appears deliberate. Renting avoids a large capital outlay and the risk of owning idle hardware. It also means the GPU count refers to machines owned by private companies, and utilisation risk sits partly with those companies when demand is low.

A later parliamentary reply filled 1 of those blanks, and left another open. [Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 2807](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU2807_hKyI1X.pdf), answered on 5 August 2026, said 15 compute service providers had been empaneled over 4 rounds, that **237 projects** had been approved for subsidised compute, and that **93.18 lakh GPU hours** (9.318 million) had been **sanctioned**. The same reply said a purchase order had been issued for a high-performance AI compute system of about 1.1 EFLOPS at the NIC data centre in Shastri Park, Delhi.

Sanctioned hours are not hours consumed. The August 2026 annex still does not print GPU-hours used, or hours per rupee released. Treat 93.18 lakh as an approval total until MeitY publishes a consumption series next to it.

A companion piece on this site, [The National AI Compute Scorecard](/compute), places India's rental model next to the US donation model, the UK purchase model, and the EU co-federation model. India can now point to sanctioned hours as well as onboarded GPUs. Consumed hours per rupee still do not appear in the annexes checked for this essay.

## Who is using the machines

Parliament also got user counts.

<Figure src="/images/essays/indiaai-users.svg?v=1" alt="IndiaAI named user slots. Researchers 114, government 58, startups 47, Fellows 8. Listed slots sum to 305." source="Rajya Sabha Q.1668" sourceUrl="https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf" wide />

305 slots across these categories. 33,099 committed GPUs. More than 100 GPUs for every named user slot. The capacity is pooled and shared, so the ratio does not mean each user holds 100 GPUs. The utilisation figure, 7,418 GPUs in use, is the closer measure of demand.

The single largest category is academic researchers, 114 of them. IndiaAI Fellows, the programme's own flagship cohort, number 8. [Takshashila warned in April 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/04/223-indiaai-mission-compute-capacity-underused-takshashila-report/) that strict eligibility rules and slow processes would leave Mission compute underused, and ranked India last among the US, China, and the EU for AI governance transparency.

Providers can report idle capacity while approved users wait on paperwork. A programme that measures onboarded GPUs will look full. A programme that measured wait time from application to first hour would look different. IndiaAI has not published that wait-time series.

## The Sarvam deal

The largest single deal in the Mission's first 2 years is Sarvam. The company had already shipped 2 open-source models before the government selected it. Sarvam-1 at 2 billion parameters and Sarvam-M at 24 billion. The selection, announced on 26 April 2025, asks Sarvam to build a sovereign open-source model at 120 billion parameters, with use cases named "2047: Citizen Connect" and "AI4Pragati" ([PIB, 30 May 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817)). The company also works with UIDAI on generative AI for Aadhaar services ([PIB, 12 Oct 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092)). 12 teams had been shortlisted for foundation models by the [February 2026 Rajya Sabha reply](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf).

Sarvam received the largest compute subsidy in the programme. [MediaNama, citing Economic Times](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-govt-minority-stake-sarvam-indiaai-mission/), reported the terms.

</div>

<p class="table-source">Source: <a href="https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-govt-minority-stake-sarvam-indiaai-mission/">Economic Times, via MediaNama</a>. Press-reported. Not in the Rajya Sabha PDF.</p>

The subsidy is 40% of the bill, matching the subsidy level MeitY announced in [January 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/02/223-indiaai-mission-allocation-reduced-69-percent-2024-2025-estimates/). The support is compulsorily convertible debentures, not a grant. If the CCDs convert, the government ends up with a 1-2% ownership stake in Sarvam.

The structure can be read 2 ways. As upside protection, the public keeps a share of the value it subsidised. As dilution, the startup gives up equity for compute it might have bought outright. Some IndiaAI participants have [said they do not want to give up equity for compute support](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/indiaai-startups-resist-centres-plan-to-seek-equity-for-support/articleshow/126356889.cms). The Sarvam terms, reported in June 2026, offer a concrete case of that trade-off. Neither reading is proven by the press table alone. The conversion price, licence terms, and any converted equity need a primary document.

Sarvam closed a $234 million round in June 2026, with HCLTech leading at $150 million and Khosla Ventures and Peak XV participating, at a valuation of about $1.5 billion ([MediaNama, June 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-govt-minority-stake-sarvam-indiaai-mission/)). The round closed days before [US export controls limited access to Anthropic's frontier models](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-anthropic-fable-5-mythos-5-suspension-us-export-controls/). Timing does not prove cause. It does show why sovereign model capacity became a live commercial topic in the same quarter.

In January 2026, Tamil Nadu signed a [₹10,000 crore MoU with Sarvam](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-tamil-nadu-sarvam-ai-sovereign-ai-park/) for a sovereign AI park, roughly the size of the entire central Mission. As of mid-2026, public AI infrastructure in India runs on at least 3 money flows. The central Mission. The state MoU. Private funding rounds. Counting only the central cash understates the country's AI spend. Counting MoUs as cash overstates it. The discipline is to keep each ledger separate.

A related piece on this site, [Sovereign AI Debt](/aidebt), treats the Sarvam CCD as the smaller cousin of sovereign funds that finance AI compute with equity, convertibles, and bank debt. The accountability question is the same at both scales. What happens to the public interest if the asset underperforms.

## What the Mission has actually delivered

The Mission has shipped real things. The list belongs next to the money and utilisation numbers, not instead of them.

**[Foundation models](/glossary/foundation-model).** MeitY received 506 proposals by 30 April 2025 ([PIB, 30 May 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817)). The [5 August 2026 Lok Sabha reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/lsapps/loksabhaquestions/annex/188/AU2807_hKyI1X.pdf) says that, against those 506 applications, 20 indigenous foundation-model proposals had been identified for support: 12 large multimodal models and 8 small language models, with intellectual property retained by the applicants. The same reply lists "notable outputs already launched": Sarvam AI's 30 billion and 105 billion parameter models, and Indus; Gnani.AI's speech-to-speech model; BharatGen's Param2-17B, Patram-7B, and Shrutam-2; and Avataar AI's video generation model. Those names and sizes are the ministry's list as of that answer. They are not an independent eval table. An earlier PIB note described Sarvam's selection as a 120 billion parameter effort. The August 2026 annex lists 30 billion and 105 billion as launched. This essay does not collapse those figures into one model. They may be successive releases, parallel products, or a change of target. The annex does not say which.

Announcement volume is high. Release quality is harder to score from PIB alone. A fair public scorecard would list model name, parameter count, open weights or not, evaluation set, and licence. That table does not yet exist in the parliamentary annex.

**Applications.** 30 applications were approved for India-specific AI use cases by July 2025, in healthcare, agriculture, climate change, governance, and assistive learning ([PIB, 12 Oct 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092)). The August 2026 Lok Sabha reply cites 62 AI prototypes developed and 20 AI solutions deployed across public-sector institutions. Approval, prototype, and deployment are 3 different stages. The annex does not list live users per application.

**AIKosh.** MeitY said 367 datasets were on the platform in May 2025 ([PIB, 30 May 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817)). By July 2025, the platform reported over 3,000 datasets and 243 AI models across 20 sectors, with 265,000 visits, 6,000 registered users, and 13,000 downloads ([PIB, 12 Oct 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092)). Count alone is not quality. [MediaNama has noted](https://www.medianama.com/2024/12/223-indiaai-mission-expenditure-budget/) that MeitY has not published a scrub protocol matching the privacy risk of residual identifiers in "AI-ready" public datasets. A dataset library can grow fast if ingestion is light. Trust grows when scrub rules are public and audited.

**FutureSkills.** More than 200 students had received fellowships by July 2025, according to [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092). 26 institutes had onboarded PhD students. 27 Data and AI Labs were set up with NIELIT, and states and Union Territories nominated 174 ITIs and polytechnics. The [Rajya Sabha reply of 13 February 2026](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf) says more than 8,000 undergraduates, 5,000 postgraduates, and 500 PhD students "are supported" for talent development, and that 27 labs have been established with 543 more identified. Those larger figures are the government's claim of students supported under the Mission, not a separate verified enrolment census published with names and institutes.

A later account, in [ThePrint on 7 August 2026](https://theprint.in/tech/ai-mission-misses-fellowship-targets-by-miles-spends-just-32-of-funds-parliament-panel-report-reveals/3008626/), citing the Thirty-First Report of the Standing Committee on Communications and Information Technology (presented to Lok Sabha and laid in Rajya Sabha on 6 August 2026), gives selection counts rather than "supported" counts. According to that reporting of MeitY's action-taken replies: in 2024 the ministry selected 150 undergraduate fellows against a target of 5,000, 140 postgraduate fellows against 2,000, and 3 PhD fellows against 100. In 2025 the UG target rose to 8,000 with 159 selected, the PG target rose to 5,000 with 101 selected, and PhD selections were 199 against a target of 167. This essay did not obtain the committee PDF itself. Treat the fellowship table as press-reported from a parliamentary report until the report is checked line by line. "Supported" and "selected" are different words. They should not be added together.

ThePrint, citing the same report, also wrote that MeitY had spent 32% of 2025-26 revised-estimate funds as on 31 December 2025. That is a spend ratio against RE, with a December cut-off. It sits beside, and is not the same as, the ₹379.15 crore **received** by IndiaAI as on 9 February 2026 in Q.1668. Received and spent can differ. The 2 dates differ. Enrolment is not a labour-market outcome. The Mission can count seats. Employers will count skills.

**Startup Financing.** The IndiaAI Startups Global programme launched in March 2025, sending 10 Indian startups to Europe with Station F and HEC Paris ([PIB, 12 Oct 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092)). Details of who actually received the money appear thinner than the GPU price annex. 10 startups seem unlikely to absorb a ₹1,942 crore line. Either the line is mostly unspent, or the disbursement list is unpublished. Both are possible. Only a spend table settles it.

**Safe & Trusted AI.** 8 projects were selected in the first round, covering machine unlearning, bias mitigation, and explainability. More than 400 applications came in for the second round. An expression of interest for partner institutions in the IndiaAI [Safety](/glossary/ai-safety) Institute was published on 9 May 2025 ([PIB, 12 Oct 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092)). The August 2026 Lok Sabha reply puts the selected-project count at 13. The pillar's outlay is still ₹20.46 crore in the February annex. That is the smallest line in the Mission. Whether that ratio is the right policy choice is a separate argument. The budget table is the fact.

**AIRAWAT** at C-DAC, the prior flagship AI platform, [suffered 3 months of downtime](https://www.medianama.com/2024/11/223-all-is-not-well-with-it-ministry-ai-platform-airawat/) in late 2024, disrupting startups that depended on it. **Bhashini**, launched in July 2022, has crossed 1 million downloads and supports 20 Indian languages with more than 350 [translation](/glossary/machine-translation) models ([PIB, 12 Oct 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092)). Neither replaces the IndiaAI money. Adding their GPU numbers to IndiaAI's would count the national total twice. Keeping them adjacent is useful. Prior programmes show both delivery and operational risk.

## A balanced reading of the design

It is too early to call the Mission a failure. It is also too early to treat the Cabinet outlay as proof of a buildout. The documents support a narrower reading.

Renting rather than buying GPUs appears to be a deliberate design choice. When utilisation is low, the machines sit idle at the providers, and the providers end up bearing much of the cost of the unused capacity. The government keeps reporting the number of GPUs onboarded. That reporting choice is rational for a rental programme. It is incomplete for a citizen who wants to know whether public subsidy bought hours of work.

Private money dwarfs the Mission. Amazon and Microsoft have committed [over $50 billion to India's cloud and AI infrastructure](https://www.medianama.com/2025/12/223-amazon-microsoft-52b-india-ai-investment/), far more than IndiaAI has received. That is company money, separate from the Mission total. The ₹10,372 crore Mission pays for access to the machines, and the machines stay with the companies. If the policy goal is cheaper access for Indian researchers and startups, rental plus subsidy can work. If the policy goal is sovereign ownership of silicon, rental cannot deliver it. The documents do not always say which goal is primary.

India's most successful digital programmes reported completed transactions. UPI payments and Aadhaar authentications. That argument is developed in the [API States](/apis) and [bureaucracy](/bureaucracy) essays on this site. IndiaAI reports onboarded GPUs, Cabinet outlays, and, as of August 2026, sanctioned GPU-hours. It has not yet reported GPU-hours **used** per rupee in the annexes checked here. Transaction metrics force a programme to show use. Capacity and sanction metrics show readiness and approval.

A timing defence exists too. 23 months into 60 is early. Foundation models take time. Empanelment takes tenders. Budget releases take Finance Ministry cycles. A Mission that spent the full outlay in year 1 would look reckless. A Mission that cannot explain why releases lag even the Revised Estimates looks opaque. Both can sit in the same paragraph.

Parliament published how the ₹10,372 crore was split across pillars. It has not published how the ₹400.94 crore was spent. The table is the plan. It is not a record of payments.

The ₹10,372 crore figure will keep appearing in speeches. By 9 February 2026, IndiaAI had received ₹400.94 crore. The 5 August 2026 Lok Sabha annex added sanctioned hours and project counts. It did not reprint a new cash-received total.
