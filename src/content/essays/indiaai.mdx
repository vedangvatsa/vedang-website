In March 2024, the [Union Cabinet](https://pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2012375) approved the IndiaAI Mission with a five-year financial outlay of **₹10,371.92 crore**. The press copy framed it as national compute capacity, indigenous foundation models, datasets, skilling, startup finance, and "Safe & Trusted AI." Twenty-three months later, a [Rajya Sabha reply](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf) (Unstarred Question 1668, answered 13 February 2026) showed how much cash had actually reached IndiaAI, the MeitY implementation arm: **₹21.79 crore** in FY 2024-25 and **₹379.15 crore** in FY 2025-26 as of 9 February 2026. That is **₹400.94 crore**, or **3.9%** of the headline outlay.

<Figure src="/images/essays/indiaai.svg?v=1" alt="Side-by-side comparison of IndiaAI Mission Cabinet outlay of 10372 crore rupees versus 401 crore rupees received by IndiaAI as of February 2026" wide />

The investigative question is not whether India should fund AI. It is what "₹10,372 crore" meant in practice: a multi-year ceiling, a marketing number, or a spending plan with matching releases. Parliament's own numbers resolve that ambiguity. The ceiling is real. The cash trail, through early 2026, is thin.

This chronology matters because IndiaAI's public communications often collapse three different clocks into one: the Cabinet approval clock, the GPU onboarding clock, and the cash-release clock. They do not move together.

## The Ledger, Not the Slogan

IndiaAI is an Independent Business Division under the [Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY)](https://www.meity.gov.in/). The Mission's seven pillars, plus a 1% overhead line, were disclosed in parliamentary answers and compiled by [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2024/12/223-indiaai-mission-expenditure-budget/) from MeitY's Lok Sabha response, then restated in the February 2026 Rajya Sabha annex.

<IndiaAIPillarAllocation />

Three facts jump from that table.

**First, compute dominates.** IndiaAI Compute Capacity is allocated **₹4,563.36 crore**, about **44%** of the Mission. That matches the political product: GPUs as industrial policy.

**Second, safety is a rounding error.** Safe & Trusted AI is allocated **₹20.46 crore**. Overheads and contingency are **₹102.69 crore**, roughly five times the safety pillar. That ratio is in the same annex that lists the pillars. It is not an editorial invention.

**Third, the "Innovation Centre" label shifted.** Early coverage listed **₹1,971.37 crore** under "IndiaAI Innovation Centre (IAIC)." Later MeitY language in the Rajya Sabha reply labels the same amount **IndiaAI Foundation Models**. Same rupee line, clearer purpose: pay for indigenous model builds.

Allocations are not expenditures. The cash that IndiaAI actually received is the harder number.

<IndiaAIFundsReleased />

Against revised estimates of ₹173 crore in FY 2024-25, IndiaAI received ₹21.79 crore (**12.6%** of that year's RE). Against RE of ₹800 crore in FY 2025-26, it had received ₹379.15 crore by 9 February 2026 (**47.4%** of RE, with the year still open). The FY 2026-27 budget estimate listed in the same reply is ₹1,000 crore, with no amount received yet as of that disclosure.

[MediaNama's April 2026 reporting](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-indiaai-mission-400-crore-over-rs-10000-crore-5-year-outlay-released/) framed this as roughly ₹400 crore released against a ₹10,000+ crore five-year story. The annex supports that framing. Verification date for the cash figures in this essay: **August 2026**, citing the 9 February 2026 cut-off in the reply.

## What "38,000 GPUs" Means

The Mission's most repeated metric is GPU count. The original Cabinet pitch, per [PIB](https://pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2012375), targeted **10,000 or more GPUs** through public-private partnerships. By May 2025, [PIB Press Release 2132817](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817) announced that an additional 15,916 GPUs on top of 18,417 empaneled units produced a total of **34,333 GPUs**. An October 2025 [PIB explainer](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092) rounded the onboarded figure to **over 38,000 GPUs**. The February 2026 Rajya Sabha reply repeats "more than 38 thousand GPUs for common compute facility have been onboarded."

Onboarding is not ownership, and it is not utilisation.

IndiaAI's model is cloud empanelment. Private providers commit GPU inventory. IndiaAI routes approved users. On the cost split, [Economic Times reporting](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/underused-gpus-raise-questions-about-indiaai-capacity-build-out/articleshow/128981840.cms) on the programme describes a structure in which IndiaAI pays about **40%** of utilisation cost and the end user pays about **60%**. That 40/60 split is a press-attributed programme mechanic, not a line in the Rajya Sabha annex; treat it as reported until MeitY reprints it in a primary disclosure. The annex does confirm that an End User Policy governs access and subsidy allocation, and that eligible users include startups, MSMEs, researchers, academic institutions, students, and government entities.

The Rajya Sabha reply states that **fourteen** AI service providers had been empaneled, with data centres in Mumbai, Navi Mumbai, Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Noida, and Jamnagar, discoverable via the [IndiaAI Compute Portal](https://compute.indiaai.gov.in).

The May 2025 PIB note named seven bidders offering commercials in that round: Cyfuture India, Ishan Infotech, Locuz Enterprise Solutions, Netmagic IT Services, Sify Digital Services, Vensysco Technologies, and Yotta Data Services. Their L1 prices are public. Those names are system integrators and Indian cloud operators, not a sovereign chip fab. The Mission buys **access** to silicon that private balance sheets financed.

<IndiaAIGPUPriceTable />

The Mission's consumer-facing slogan is GPUs at "**₹65 per hour**," repeated in the [October 2025 PIB piece](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092). The tender table shows why that number is technically true and strategically incomplete. Entry-level SKUs (NVIDIA L4, Intel Gaudi 2, L40S) clear near or below that band on one-month pricing. An H100 SXM at L1 is **₹153/hour** on-demand and **₹134.10/hour** on one-month pricing. A B200 SXM is **₹323/hour** on-demand. Subsidies can pull end-user prices down further. The headline ₹65 figure is a floor for light SKUs, not the cost of training-class clusters.

## The Utilisation Gap

Capacity announcements travel farther than utilisation data. On utilisation, two layers of evidence exist.

**Primary (Parliament):** The [Rajya Sabha annex](https://www.medianama.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/annex_270_AU1668_ajohnN.pdf) lists who was availing compute "as on date" of the reply:

<IndiaAIUserCounts />

These are small absolute numbers next to a 38,000-GPU headline. Academic researchers (114) outnumber startups and MSMEs (47). Government entities (58) are a large share of the disclosed mix. The reply does not publish a utilisation percentage.

**Secondary (press, citing an IndiaAI letter to CSPs):** [The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/underused-gpus-raise-questions-about-indiaai-capacity-build-out/articleshow/128981840.cms) reported that of **33,099** GPUs committed by providers, IndiaAI had assigned **12,638** (**38%**) to workloads, and only **7,418** (**22%**) were utilised by end users, with **5,317** described as unused committed units in that communication. This essay treats those ET figures as **reported**, not as a MeitY statistical series, until the same numbers appear in a parliamentary paper.

<IndiaAIComputeFunnel />

The underuse risk was predicted early. The [Takshashila Institution's State of AI Governance work](https://legion.takshashila.org.in/research/state-of-ai-governance), covered by [MediaNama in April 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/04/223-indiaai-mission-compute-capacity-underused-takshashila-report/), argued that subsidy eligibility and bureaucratic friction would leave compute underutilised even if hardware arrived. The later CSP letter, as reported, is consistent with that prediction. Correlation is not proof of causation. The direction of the evidence is consistent: **announced capacity >> assigned capacity >> used capacity**.

A GPU that is empaneled, assigned, and idle is three different policy objects. IndiaAI's public scoreboard mostly counts the first.
</PullQuote>

## Where the Money Touches Models

Compute is the largest pillar. Foundation Models (**₹1,971.37 crore** allocated) is the narrative pillar: "Making AI in India."

[PIB's 30 May 2025 release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817) states that **506 proposals** were received by 30 April 2025 under the Foundation Model call. On 26 April 2025, **Sarvam AI** was selected to build India's sovereign LLM stack, described as an open-source **120 billion** parameter effort, following Sarvam-1 (2B) and Sarvam-M (24B). Three additional selections were announced in the same release:

| Company | Stated target (per PIB) |
|---------|-------------------------|
| [Soket AI](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132817) | Open-source 120B foundation model for linguistic diversity |
| Gnani AI | 14B Voice AI foundation model |
| Gan AI | 70B multilingual model aimed at "Superhuman TTS" |

By the February 2026 Rajya Sabha reply, MeitY said **twelve teams** had been shortlisted for indigenous foundational models. The October 2025 PIB explainer had listed four first-phase startups (Sarvam, Soket, Gnani, Gan) and noted **thirty** applications approved under the Application Development Initiative by July 2025.

### Sarvam: subsidy, CCDs, and a reported equity stub

The cleanest money trail into a private model builder, as covered by [MediaNama citing Economic Times](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-govt-minority-stake-sarvam-indiaai-mission/), is Sarvam's compute support under the Mission: a reported subsidy of **₹98.68 crore** against a total compute bill of **₹246.71 crore**, for access to **4,096 NVIDIA H100** GPUs for six months. According to that reporting, support was structured as compulsorily convertible debentures (CCDs), expected to convert into a **1-2%** minority government stake around Sarvam's then-ongoing funding round (press reports placed the round near **$300 million** at about a **$1.5 billion** valuation).

Those Sarvam commercial terms are **press-sourced**, not reproduced here from a MeitY PDF. They are material enough to flag, and too important to present as audited fact without the underlying instrument. Qualified language applies until primary filings or a parliamentary annex confirm the CCD terms.

The policy implication does not require the exact equity percentage. Public money is flowing into private foundation-model capacity through **compute subsidy**, not only through classic grants. That is a different accountability surface than a pure R&D grant: utilisation hours, residual ownership, and open-source delivery conditions all matter.

## Datasets, Skills, Safety: The Thin Edges

**AIKosh (Datasets Platform)** is allocated **₹199.55 crore**. The October 2025 PIB explainer claimed **over 3,000 datasets** and **243 AI models** across 20 sectors, with more than 265,000 visits, 6,000 registered users, and 13,000 downloads by July 2025. Live catalogue counts move; the [AIKosh portal](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/) is the primary surface to re-check. Dataset *count* is not dataset *quality*. MeitY's early replies emphasised making public-sector datasets "AI-ready" without publishing a verification protocol commensurate with the privacy risk of residual identifiers, a point [MediaNama raised](https://www.medianama.com/2024/12/223-indiaai-mission-expenditure-budget/) when the pillar split first appeared.

**FutureSkills** is allocated **₹882.94 crore**. The Rajya Sabha reply states support for more than **8,000** undergraduates, **5,000** postgraduates, and **500** PhD students, plus **27** India Data and AI labs established and **543** more identified. Those are enrolment and network metrics. They are not yet labour-market outcome metrics.

**Safe & Trusted AI** remains the structural oddity: **₹20.46 crore** against a Mission that markets responsible AI. PIB materials describe project rounds on machine unlearning, bias mitigation, privacy-preserving ML, explainability, and an IndiaAI Safety Institute expression of interest. Ambition language is abundant. Budget weight is not.

**Startup Financing** is the second-largest non-compute pillar at **₹1,942.5 crore**, almost equal to Foundation Models. Public detail on *how* that money moves (grants vs equity vs loans vs credit guarantees) remains thinner than the compute tender trail. The October 2025 PIB explainer highlights the [IndiaAI Startups Global](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178092) programme launched March 2025 with Station F and HEC Paris, helping **ten** Indian startups expand into Europe. Ten startups is a pilot cohort, not a ₹1,942 crore absorption plan. Until MeitY publishes instrument-level disbursements for this pillar, the financing line is best read as **allocated capacity**, not proven cheque flow. That opacity is the same class of problem MediaNama flagged when the pillar split first appeared: large rupee labels without matching disbursement mechanics.

Taken together, the thin edges share a pattern. Datasets are counted. Students are counted. Safety projects are counted. Startup programmes are announced. The Mission's hardest-currency disclosures remain the Cabinet outlay, the year-wise cash to IndiaAI, and the GPU commercial tables. Everything else still needs a second source.

## What This Is Not

Two adjacent programmes get conflated with IndiaAI in public debate. They should be separated for ledger integrity.

**[AIRAWAT](https://airawat-psai.cdac.in/airawat/)** at C-DAC Pune is a MeitY-linked AI supercomputing platform (200 AI petaflops; Top500 ranking reported at launch). It is not the same object as IndiaAI's empaneled commercial GPU cloud. Mixing AIRAWAT FLOPs with IndiaAI "onboarded GPUs" double-counts national compute.

**[Bhashini](https://bhashini.gov.in/)** (National Language Translation Mission) is a separate MeitY language stack. It appears in AI narrative packages and MoUs, including railway multilingual deployments noted in PIB explainers. It is not a line item that replaces the IndiaAI pillar table.

Separating brands matters because Cabinet outlays are easy to sum rhetorically and hard to sum in cash.

## Public Ceiling, Private Capex

Even if IndiaAI eventually spends its full ₹10,371.92 crore, that number sits beside much larger private infrastructure commitments that Indian and global cloud operators announce in dollars. [MediaNama's reading](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-indiaai-mission-400-crore-over-rs-10000-crore-5-year-outlay-released/) of the February 2026 reply noted that firms such as Amazon and Microsoft have publicly discussed India cloud and AI infrastructure plans measured in tens of billions of dollars, orders of magnitude above the Mission's cash releases to date. Those corporate figures are company announcements, not audited IndiaAI spend, and they should not be added into the Mission ledger. They do change the power map: whoever owns the racks and power purchase agreements shapes who can train, at what latency, and under what data-residency terms.

That is why the empaneled-CSP design is not a footnote. It is the architecture. IndiaAI's Compute pillar budget (**₹4,563.36 crore** allocated) is, in practice, a subsidy and routing budget layered on private inventory. When utilisation lags, private operators hold idle capital expenditure while the government holds a political GPU count. Both sides then have an incentive to announce the next capacity target before publishing the utilisation of the last one.

This is the [bureaucracy tax](/bureaucracy) in silicon form: eligibility portals, short lease windows, and multi-step approvals that raise the effective cost of "cheap" GPUs. Takshashila's underuse warning was specifically about that friction meeting thin eligible demand. The parliamentary beneficiary table (hundreds of slots, not tens of thousands of active training jobs) is the empirical check.

India's stronger digital programmes, documented in the [API States](/apis) and [agentic state](/stateagents) essays, succeeded when the unit of accountability was a completed transaction (a UPI payment, an Aadhaar authentication) rather than a pledged machine. IndiaAI will clear that bar when MeitY publishes, on a fixed cadence: (1) cash released by pillar, (2) GPU hours consumed by SKU class, (3) model eval scores for publicly funded builds, and (4) dataset licences with scrub audits.

## The Structural Reading

Put the verified pieces on one page:

1. **Outlay ≠ appropriation ≠ release.** ₹10,371.92 crore is a five-year ceiling. Annual RE figures are much smaller. Cash received by IndiaAI through early February 2026 was about ₹401 crore.
2. **Empaneled ≠ utilised.** 38,000+ GPUs is an onboarding metric. Parliamentary beneficiary counts are in the low hundreds of slots. Press-reported utilisation of committed GPUs sits near one-fifth.
3. **Safety is underweighted on paper.** ₹20.46 crore for Safe & Trusted AI versus ₹102.69 crore for overheads is an explicit design choice in the disclosed split.
4. **Private cloud capex is doing heavy lifting.** The Mission buys access and subsidy, not a sovereign warehouse of chips. Providers such as Yotta, CyFuture, Netmagic, and Sify appear in PIB price tables as L1 bidders. Their balance sheets, not the Consolidated Fund alone, hold much of the silicon.
5. **Foundation-model policy is converging on a few firms.** Sarvam's selection and reported compute subsidy illustrate concentration. Twelve shortlisted teams (per the February 2026 reply) is still a narrow funnel relative to 506 proposals.

This pattern is familiar from other Indian digital programmes: a large Cabinet number establishes political seriousness; implementation agencies then fight the annual budget cycle; private partners supply the physical layer; dashboards optimise for countable inputs (GPUs onboarded, labs identified, proposals received). India's [API-state](/apis) successes (UPI, Aadhaar authentication volumes) were different because the metric that mattered was **production transactions**, not pledged capacity. IndiaAI will deserve the same standard when MeitY publishes utilisation and model evals with the same frequency as GPU headcount.

## What Remains Unverified

Intellectual honesty requires a short list of open items:

- **Pillar-wise expenditure** (not just outlay) has not been published in the same annex that lists pillar allocations. The Rajya Sabha question asked for pillar-wise releases; the answer provided year-wise cash to IndiaAI and pillar-wise *outlays*, not a pillar-wise spend table.
- **Sarvam CCD terms and stake percentage** remain press-reported as of this writing.
- **CSP utilisation letter** figures (33,099 / 12,638 / 7,418) are attributed via [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/underused-gpus-raise-questions-about-indiaai-capacity-build-out/articleshow/128981840.cms); they should be reconciled to a MeitY primary release.
- **Post-February 2026 releases** may have moved the ₹400.94 crore cash total. Re-check the next parliamentary session's annexes before treating that sum as permanent.
- **Startup Financing mechanics** (₹1,942.5 Cr outlay) lack a public instrument-by-instrument disbursement table comparable to the GPU L1 price annex.
- **Application Development** outcomes beyond "30 applications approved" (per PIB / Rajya Sabha talking points) are not yet published as production deployment metrics with ministry sign-off.

Researchers who want to extend this ledger should start from three URLs and refuse to leave them: the [IndiaAI Mission hub](https://indiaai.gov.in/), the [Compute Portal](https://compute.indiaai.gov.in), and the latest MeitY annex uploaded to [sansad.in](https://sansad.in/) or mirrored by [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/). Everything else is commentary.

<KeyTakeaway>
IndiaAI's binding constraint in its first two years was not imagination. It was the distance between a ₹10,372 crore Cabinet headline and ₹401 crore of received cash, compounded by a compute programme that counts onboarded GPUs faster than it proves they are busy.
</KeyTakeaway>

The Mission can still succeed. Empaneled capacity, a live compute portal, foundation-model selections, and AIKosh are real institutional facts. So is the ledger. Until releases catch the rhetoric, and until utilisation is published beside onboarding, "₹10,372 crore for AI" remains a political sentence that Parliament has already begun to fact-check.
