On 12 June 2026 an OpenAlex pull tagged **128,286** papers as [blockchain](https://openalex.org/C2779687700) or [cryptocurrency](https://openalex.org/C180706569) between 2013 and mid-2026. Yearly output in that file went from 176 papers in 2013 to 20,668 in 2025. That is 117 times more papers in twelve years. The tables are in [Web3 Research Trends](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6990919) on SSRN.

The same tag filter, run on 16 August 2026, returned **133,681** papers for 2013 to 2026. That is 5,395 extra records in nine weeks. OpenAlex says [concepts are deprecated](https://developers.openalex.org/guides/deprecations): the tags still return results, and nobody updates them. The current tag, topic [T10270](https://openalex.org/T10270) ("Blockchain Technology Applications and Security"), returns **211,641** papers for the same years.

I use the June file as a count from that day. Then I check it against live chains, court filings, and statutes. A paper count shows what people wrote. It does not show what still runs.

## How to read a paper count

Five counts get mixed. Mix them and a keyword jump reads like a boom, or a tracker row reads like a live currency.

1. **June snapshot.** Papers with those two concept tags, pulled on 12 June 2026. Headline 128,286. This essay's charts. A count from that day.
2. **Live concepts.** The same tags, queried later. On 16 August 2026: 133,681. OpenAlex [will keep serving concepts](https://developers.openalex.org/api-reference/concepts) and will not update them. About 65,000 concept labels, carried over from Microsoft Academic Graph.
3. **Live topics.** OpenAlex's current tags, built with CWTS. About 4,500 topics. T10270 for 2013 to 2026: 211,641. A different set. Not a fix of the June file.
4. **Keyword net.** A wider abstract search in the June paper: 1,938,409 papers. An upper bound. Not the tagged set.
5. **Live systems.** Value locked, court dockets, statutes, CBDC trackers, shipping platforms. These do not move when abstracts do.

[Priem, Piwowar, and Orr (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01833) built OpenAlex as an open replacement for Microsoft Academic Graph. How to cite it is on the [help page](https://help.openalex.org/hc/en-us/articles/28761511652247-How-can-I-cite-OpenAlex). Run the same query later and the digits will change. That is how the index works.

The two concept tags overlap. On 16 August, for 2013 to 2026, blockchain alone was 102,916 papers and cryptocurrency alone was 40,178. Add them and you get 143,094. The combined set is 133,681. **9,413 papers carry both tags.** The `works_count` figures on the tag pages (122,694 and 71,167) cover all years, not 2013 to 2026. Do not add those two figures and call the sum the field.

The yearly bars in the June chart add up to **127,286**. The paper's tagged total is **128,286**. The 1,000-record gap is in the snapshot. The 16 August yearly buckets add up exactly to 133,681. I cannot close the 1,000 from the published year splits. Use 128,286 as the paper's headline. Use the yearly bars as the published year splits.

The June paper also checked other databases as coverage, not as copies. Crossref 124,333 (within 3% of the June file). Europe PMC 61,191. arXiv 11,312. DBLP 37,246. Those four figures are from the 12 June pull. I did not re-run them on 16 August. Journal articles are 68.9% of the tagged set, book chapters 13.9%, preprints 8.2%. Open access is 50.2% here. [Piwowar, Priem, Larivière, et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.4375) put all-field Crossref journal articles at 28% open access. That 28% is 2018, all fields.

Earlier counts were smaller because the field was smaller. [Miau and Yang (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1080/09537325.2018.1434138) stopped in 2017. The June file is about 100 times larger than those studies. A larger file is still a file of papers.

## What the papers were about, year by year

**2013 to 2016.** 176 to 955 papers. The object was still [Nakamoto (2008)](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf): stop double-spending without a bank. [Lamport, Shostak, and Pease (1982)](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/byz.pdf) asked how generals agree when some messengers lie. Those four years in the chart sum to 2,111 papers, about five weeks of 2025 output.

**2017 to 2019.** 2017 grew 164% to 2,520. 2018 grew 178% to 6,995, the fastest year in the June file. [Buterin's Ethereum whitepaper](https://ethereum.org/en/whitepaper/) and [Wood's Yellow Paper](https://ethereum.github.io/yellowpaper/paper.pdf) turned a ledger into a computer that stores state. [Androulaki et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3190508.3190538) described Hyperledger Fabric for firms that wanted a chain only invited members can write to. By 2019 the yearly count was 10,591.

**2020 to now.** Growth slowed. 2024 grew 1.8%. Papers from 2020 through mid-2026 are 82.7% of the June file (106,069 of 128,286). The 2026 slice in that share is 8,419 papers seen by 12 June, not a full year. Ethereum's Merge on 15 September 2022 ended proof-of-work. The old Foundation blog post for that day now [404s](https://blog.ethereum.org/2022/09/15/the-merge-is-here). The live page is [ethereum.org/roadmap/merge](https://ethereum.org/roadmap/merge/). [ethereum.org's energy page](https://ethereum.org/en/energy-consumption/) cites CCRI: about 2,601 MWh a year after the switch (0.0026 TWh), a drop of more than 99.988% in electricity and about 99.992% in carbon against proof-of-work. Those are estimates. The page says they are not an official Foundation promise. The comparison table on that page, figures accessed July 2023, puts Bitcoin at 149 TWh/yr, about 53,000 times proof-of-stake Ethereum. Bitcoin demand moves daily. The live index is the [Cambridge Bitcoin Electricity Consumption Index](https://ccaf.io/cbnsi/cbeci).

## Supply chain is the big non-finance topic. The big shipping product shut down.

"Blockchain" is in 68.5% of June abstracts (87,818). "Security" is in 46.3% (59,388). [Zheng, Xie, Dai, Chen, and Wang (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1504/IJWGS.2018.095647) listed failure modes early. [Khan and Salah (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.future.2017.11.022) did the same for IoT. Security stayed in the abstracts. "Smart contract" is in 29.9% (38,414). "Bitcoin" is in 27.9%.

<Figure src="/images/essays/web3-terms.svg?v=2" alt="Top two-word phrases in the June 2026 tagged set. Blockchain technology 44,102. Smart contract 21,134. Supply chain 11,418." source="SSRN 6990919" sourceUrl="https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6990919" width={400} height={380} />

"Blockchain technology" (44,102) and "blockchain based" (18,516) are how authors add a ledger to some other system. "Supply chain" is the largest non-finance job at 11,418. [Saberi, Kouhizadeh, Sarkis, and Shen (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1080/00207543.2018.1533261) tied it to sustainability claims. [Christidis and Devetsikiotis (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2016.2566339) tied it to IoT.

The largest industry test of that claim was TradeLens, a Maersk and IBM shipping platform announced in 2018. On 29 November 2022, [Maersk said it would shut the platform](https://www.maersk.com/news/articles/2022/11/29/maersk-and-ibm-to-discontinue-tradelens). The [19 December 2022 Wayback capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20221219103843/https://www.maersk.com/news/articles/2022/11/29/maersk-and-ibm-to-discontinue-tradelens) still has the text. The companies wrote that they had built a working platform, but "the need for full global industry collaboration has not been achieved," and it had not reached "the level of commercial viability necessary to continue work and meet the financial expectations as an independent business." The plan was to go offline by the end of Q1 2023. Supply-chain papers kept arriving after that. The June file does not subtract dead products.

Health and privacy have early papers and smaller counts. [Zyskind, Nathan, and Pentland (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1109/SPW.2015.27) stored pointers to personal data, not the data. [Ekblaw, Azaria, Halamka, and Lippman](https://doi.org/10.1109/OBD.2016.11) built MedRec. "Healthcare" is in 13.7% of abstracts. "Machine learning" (5,667) plus "artificial intelligence" (5,463) is 11,130 mentions. [Kosba, Miller, Shi, Wen, and Papamanthou (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1109/SP.2016.55) hid contract internals (Hawk). [Lu, Huang, Zhang, Maharjan, and Zhang (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVT.2020.2973651) put federated learning on a ledger for vehicles. Those papers exist. 11,130 mentions is not 11,130 deployments.

## DeFi papers rose. Locked dollars did not move as one line.

DeFi mentions: 7 papers in 2019, 12 in 2020, 63 in 2021, **516 in 2025**. That is 74 times in six years. NFT mentions: 60 in 2021, **426 in 2023**, 334 in 2024, 366 in 2025.

<Figure src="/images/essays/web3-defi-nft.svg?v=2" alt="DeFi abstract mentions 7 in 2019 to 516 in 2025. NFT mentions peaked at 426 in 2023." source="SSRN 6990919" sourceUrl="https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6990919" width={400} height={300} />

[Uniswap's docs](https://docs.uniswap.org) still describe the pool that replaced the order book. Live value locked is a different meter. [DefiLlama](https://defillama.com) is the public index. Its [2025 sector note](https://defillama.com/research/report/state-of-defi-2025) does not give one number for "DeFi." It splits:

- Lending TVL rose from $48.15 billion to $64.06 billion inside 2025. Top ten protocols held $57.00 billion, 89.0% of lending TVL.
- Liquid staking slipped from $58.73 billion to $57.62 billion.
- Restaking fell from $23.85 billion to $18.78 billion, a 21.2% drop.
- Tokenised US Treasuries and money-market products rose from $3.87 billion at end-2024 to $9.12 billion by late 2025.

A paper count can rise while some locked-dollar series rise and others fall. They are not the same series.

The algorithmic stablecoin that blew up is in a court file. It is also in abstracts. On 16 February 2023 the [SEC charged Terraform Labs and Do Kwon](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-32) with a multi-billion-dollar securities fraud around UST, LUNA, and related tokens. Enforcement Director Gurbir S. Grewal said, as alleged, "the Terraform ecosystem was neither decentralized, nor finance." On 5 April 2024 a jury found them liable for securities fraud. On 13 June 2024 the [SEC announced a package above $4.5 billion](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-73): Terraform $3,586,875,883 disgorgement, $466,952,423 interest, $420 million penalty; Kwon $110 million disgorgement and $14,320,196 interest, joint and several with Terraform, plus an $80 million penalty. That release says the May 2022 de-peg "wiped out $40 billion in market value nearly overnight." Academics kept writing about DeFi after that. They wrote about reserved dollars and over-collateralized loans, not about UST.

NFT paper volume peaked in 2023, after the speculative market had already broken. Later papers are more about who owned an item than about profile pictures. The June file does not prove NFTs "found a use." It proves the word stayed in abstracts after the floor prices died.

Stablecoins already move money. The payment claim is smaller than the chain volume. [CoinDesk, citing McKinsey and Artemis](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/01/23/stablecoins-moved-usd35-trillion-last-year-but-only-1-of-it-was-for-real-world-payments) (23 January 2026), put 2025 on-chain stablecoin volume above $35 trillion. The same article prints **$390 billion** of "genuine" payments in the summary and **$380 billion** in the body ($226 billion B2B, $90 billion payroll and remittances, $8 billion capital markets). Those two figures do not match. The finding is about 1%. The article cannot settle $380 billion vs $390 billion. McKinsey put the global payments market above $2 quadrillion, so even the higher figure is about 0.02% of that total. Circle and Tether still dominate issuance.

## Keywords that rose, and what actually shipped

Growth ratios in the June file compare 2025-2026 mentions to 2022-2023. A 6x jump off 37 papers is not the same as a 3x jump off 4,000.

<Figure src="/images/essays/web3-rising.svg?v=2" alt="Fastest-rising terms in the June file. Quantum resistant 6.6x. Real world asset 5.6x. Layer 2 4.2x. zk rollup 3.4x off 34 papers." source="SSRN 6990919" sourceUrl="https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6990919" width={400} height={420} />

**Post-quantum.** "Quantum resistant" grew 6.6x (37 to 243). "Post-quantum" grew 3.9x (95 to 367). Together, 610 papers in 2025-2026. Bitcoin and Ethereum still sign with elliptic curves. [Shor (1994)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9508027) showed a large quantum computer would break those signatures. There is no such machine on the public record. What moved the papers is a standards event. On 13 August 2024, [NIST published FIPS 203, 204, and 205](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/08/nist-releases-first-3-finalized-post-quantum-encryption-standards). [FIPS 203](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.203.pdf) is ML-KEM. The [CSRC PQC page](https://csrc.nist.gov/groups/ST/post-quantum-crypto/) tells operators to start migrating. It does not give a date when ECDSA dies.

**Scale.** "Layer 2" grew 4.2x (275 papers). "zk rollup" grew 3.4x off **34** papers. The count rose. Do not treat 3.4x as a volume story. [Buterin's 2021 rollup note](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2021/01/05/rollup.html) is still the map: execute off the base chain, post data or a proof back. [L2BEAT](https://l2beat.com/scaling/summary) tracks live rollups. The page is JavaScript. I did not copy a TVL figure from a blank HTML fetch. "Zero knowledge" grew 2.1x (743). The SSRN abstract flags that 2.1x as the fastest rise among cryptographic primitives.

**Rules and tokens.** "Regulatory" grew 3.5x and the count is large: 4,529 papers. "Real world asset" grew 5.6x off 89 papers. "Stablecoin" grew 2.7x (576). "DeFi" grew 2.6x (808). "NFT" and "metaverse" sit at 0.8x.

[BlackRock launched BUIDL](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240320771318/en) on Ethereum on 20 March 2024, a tokenized money-market fund for qualified investors, $5 million minimum, cash and Treasuries and repo, tokens targeting $1. That is a product. 89 "real world asset" papers are not 89 BUIDLs. DefiLlama's tokenised-Treasury line ($3.87 billion to $9.12 billion in 2025) is closer to live dollars.

## Laws arrived. Paper counts followed later.

The EU's [Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1114/oj) (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114) was adopted on 31 May 2023 and published on 9 June 2023. It is the first EU-wide set of rules for issuance and crypto-asset services.

The US followed on stablecoins. The [GENIUS Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582) became [Public Law 119-27 on 18 July 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ27/pdf/PLAW-119publ27.pdf). Payment stablecoins, 1:1 reserves. Signing remarks are in the [Daily Compilation](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-202500778/html/DCPD-202500778.htm). The remaining design in statute is the reserved dollar, not the algorithmic one the SEC sued.

Spot Bitcoin exchange-traded products were approved on 10 January 2024. [Chair Gensler's statement](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/gensler-statement-spot-bitcoin-011023) is the public-market event. He wrote that the Commission did not approve bitcoin itself.

## 146 countries sit on a tracker. Three launched. The largest rail is still a pilot.

The [Atlantic Council tracker](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/cbdctracker/) (May 2026) has 146 countries and currency unions with a CBDC entry, covering over 98% of world GDP, up from 87 in May 2022. 77 are in development, pilot, or launch. 41 pilots. Three retail CBDCs have launched: the Bahamas, Jamaica, and Nigeria. Every G20 member except the US is in the tracker. Canada, Australia, and Norway have stepped back from retail. The [BIS Innovation Hub CBDC page](https://www.bis.org/about/bisih/topics/cbdc.htm) covers the technical work. The [ECB digital euro page](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/digital_euro/html/index.en.html) is the euro-area project. India's [RBI concept note](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationReportDetails.aspx?ID=1218) set out an e-rupee. CBDC papers in the June file go from 1 in 2016 to 205 in 2025. They follow official work. They are not 205 live currencies.

The tracker splits "launch" from "pilot." Volume and legal status do not line up.

By December 2025, according to the same tracker, retail e-CNY had processed more than 3.4 billion transactions worth about 16.7 trillion yuan (~$2.3 trillion). In January 2026 the PBOC reclassified e-CNY as deposit liabilities. The Bahamas, Jamaica, and Nigeria are the three full launches. The largest rail is still in the pilot column.

Nigeria launched eNaira in October 2021. The IMF's [May 2023 working paper](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/wp/2023/english/wpiea2023104-print-pdf.pdf) (WP/23/104) found about 14,000 transactions a week, 98.5% of wallets unused in a given week, and average weekly value of 923 million naira, 0.0018% of M3. The Fund called public adoption "disappointingly low." A later Article IV staff report, as quoted in [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/article/three-years-in-enaira-struggles-as-transactions-crawl-to-n29bn/), put cumulative transactions at 854,512 and N29.3 billion against N611 trillion of electronic payments in Nigeria in 2023.

China is the opposite case. Cryptocurrency trading is illegal. The [PBOC and nine other departments](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/tiaofasi/144941/3581332/4348658/index.html) issued 银发〔2021〕237号 in September 2021: virtual currency has no legal-tender status, related businesses are illegal financial activity, overseas exchanges serving mainland residents are also illegal. The [NDRC mining notice](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-09/25/content_5639225.htm) (发改运行〔2021〕1283号, 3 September 2021) ordered existing mines out and cut finance and power. The e-CNY is the state substitute. [Atlantic Council, 15 January 2026](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/econographics/what-to-watch-as-china-prepares-its-digital-yuan-for-prime-time/), citing PBOC year-end data, matches the tracker on the 3.4 billion transactions and 16.7 trillion yuan. Project mBridge, after the BIS stepped back in October 2024, had processed about $55.49 billion, with e-CNY about 95% of settlement. That is not bitcoin. It is a central bank payment system.

The [Blockchain-based Service Network introductory whitepaper](https://bsnbase.io/static/tmpFile/BSNIntroductionWhitepaper.pdf) (5 February 2020) describes a network for government and firm chains that invited members write to. Chinese paper volume can be high while "Bitcoin" as a keyword is no longer the center. Inside China the legal topic is not a public coin.

## China and India write the papers. The US writes the listings.

China 17,258 papers (13.5%). India 17,037 (13.3%). United States 12,488 (9.7%). Top three: 36.5% of the June file. In most computer-science fields the US is first or second. Here it is third.

<Figure src="/images/essays/web3-countries.svg?v=2" alt="Country counts in the June file. China 17,258. India 17,037. United States 12,488." source="SSRN 6990919" sourceUrl="https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6990919" width={400} height={280} />

OpenAlex assigns a paper to every co-author country, so shares can overlap. Read this as where authors sit.

The country split matches use more than it matches famous US universities. [Chainalysis's 2023 Global Crypto Adoption Index](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/2023-global-crypto-adoption-index/) put India first in everyday use. Nigeria second, Vietnam third, the United States fourth. The [2023 geography report](https://www.chainalysis.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/the-2023-geography-of-cryptocurrency-report-release.pdf) put India second in raw estimated volume after the US, about $250 billion received in the year to June 2023. That was after [section 115BBH](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/Acts/Income-tax%20Act,%201961/2022/102120000000079592.htm) of the Income-tax Act (Finance Act 2022): 30% on transfer of a virtual digital asset, no expense other than cost of acquisition, no loss set-off. [Section 194S](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-194s-4) deducts 1% TDS. People used crypto anyway. Indonesia is rank 6 in the June paper file (3,106) and rank 7 on that adoption index. Saudi Arabia is rank 10 here (2,423).

No single US university sits in the top eight institutions in the June file. The [Chinese Academy of Sciences](https://english.cas.cn) has 924 papers. Indian Institutes of Technology occupy two of the top six slots. US work is spread. A high paper count is not a quality ranking. Citation-weighted lists can rank the same countries differently. The [State of AI essay](/stateofai) has the same warning on a five-million-paper pull.

China leads the paper count and bans the public coin. India is a close second in papers, first in everyday use, and taxes that use at 30%. The US is third in papers and first in the products that list on NYSE and Nasdaq.

## Most of these papers will not be cited

43.5% of the 128,286 papers have zero citations. 31.5% have 1 to 13. 20% have 14 to 41. 5% have 42 or more. The 99th percentile sits at 249. [Nakamoto (2008)](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf) is an outlier at 14,286 OpenAlex citations in the June pull. [Zheng et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1109/BigDataCongress.2017.85) is the survey a thousand later papers cite instead of citing the primary system. Melanie Swan's [2015 book](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/blockchain-blueprint/9781491920480/) still collects citations. The long tail of "blockchain based X for Y" does not.

82.7% of the tagged set is from 2020 on. New papers have had little time. Average citations per paper in the yearly file fall from 85.0 in 2013 to 0.8 in 2025. That is new papers plus too many papers. It is not proof that 2025 papers are worse. It is proof that citation totals lag, and that most of this file will never be read twice.

## Quote a date and a filter

If you cite 128,286 as the size of Web3 research in 2026, say the pull date and the tag. A later reader who uses topics will get 211,641 and think you padded. A later reader who uses concepts will get 133,681 and think you undercounted. Neither number is TradeLens, UST, eNaira, or e-CNY.

Supply-chain papers kept arriving after Maersk shut TradeLens. DeFi papers kept arriving after the SEC sued Terraform. Lending TVL rose in 2025 while restaking fell. NFT papers peaked after floor prices died. CBDC papers track official projects. They do not tell you that eNaira wallets sit idle, that e-CNY clears trillions of yuan while still sitting in the pilot column, or that 205 papers in 2025 are not 205 currencies.

NIST's 2024 FIPS moved post-quantum work from "whether" to "which lattice scheme." List where you still use ECDSA. A 6.6x keyword jump is not a date when those signatures stop working.

China and India write a quarter of the papers. MiCA and the GENIUS Act are the EU and US rules. If a standard or a CBDC design is going to be used, it will be used there too. The [Web3 Reports Library](/web3lib) is the live search over this literature.
